Atraxa refills your hand; she is not the entire win condition. Develop colored mana, resolve her with protection available when possible, then use blink to turn each creature into a reusable resource. Finish with a developed combat board or the three-card mana loop. A hand of expensive blink engines without lands and early acceleration is not a functional opening hand.

The Game Changers are Cyclonic Rift, Rhystic Study, and Smothering Tithe. There are 36 lands plus two land-option spells, sixteen dedicated acceleration cards, and additional mana reuse from Peregrine Drake and Tezzeret. The list is tuned for fast development into a Bracket 3 midgame, with the assembled combo treated as a turn-six-or-later finish rather than an early tutor race. It does not include a mass land denial or extra-turn plan.

Get four colors online before the seven-drop
Birds of Paradise

One-mana fixing for all four colors, then a small body for Craterhoof or Finale. Noble Hierarch performs a similar early role but cannot make black. Its exalted bonus helps an otherwise solitary Atraxa attack without increasing the number of bodies exposed in combat.

Delighted Halfling

Makes Atraxa harder to counter when its colored mana is spent casting her. That colored mana cannot pay for ordinary nonlegendary spells or your instant interaction. The colorless mode remains useful for their generic costs. Do not count Halfling as unrestricted four-color fixing.

Wild Growth

With Utopia Sprawl, supplies cheap land-based acceleration that survives creature wipes. Sprawl must enchant a Forest and needs a color chosen when it enters; choose for the hand and missing Atraxa colors. Neither enchantment grants the land an extra tap per turn.

Nature's Lore

Along with Three Visits, finds a Forest card rather than only a basic. Fetch an original dual untapped or a shockland if willing to pay life. Spara's Headquarters and Zagoth Triome still enter tapped. These spells help establish green plus the missing white, blue, or black before Atraxa. Farseek instead finds a Plains, Island, Swamp, or Mountain card and always puts it in tapped; it cannot find a plain basic Forest.

Sol Ring

Powerful generic acceleration, but Atraxa still needs GWUB. Arcane Signet supplies dependable fixing and Fellwar Stone supplies colors available from opponents' lands. Do not keep a color-poor hand solely because Ring appears in it.

Bloom Tender

With Atraxa present, taps for GWUB; earlier it only sees the colors actually among your permanents. Do not assume it makes four mana before she arrives. It becomes a useful untap target for Tezzeret after your board develops.

Faeburrow Elder

Supplies at least green and white from its own colors, then all four with Atraxa. It also contributes a vigilant body for combat. Summoning sickness matters, so this is development for the following turn rather than an immediate refund on an empty board.

Overlord of the Hauntwoods

Impending for three mana supplies a tapped Everywhere land token immediately, fixing all four colors through its basic land types. While time counters keep it a noncreature, creature-only blink cannot target it. Once active it is a 6/5 whose entries and attacks create more lands.

Invasion of Zendikar // Awakened Skyclave

Four-mana ramp for up to two basic lands and a useful battle hit for Atraxa. Four basics provide initial targets, but repeated searches can exhaust them. Its transformed creature is also a land and can tap for any color with haste. In the library it has the front face's battle type, not the back face's creature type.

Smothering Tithe

Converts opposing draws into mana unless they pay. Treasures help you cast Atraxa while retaining interaction and fund expensive finishers. It is powerful but dependent on opponents' choices; do not count speculative Treasures as guaranteed mana when planning a turn.

Resolve Atraxa and keep the cards coming
Atraxa, Grand Unifier

Reveal ten and select up to one card for each card type. These go into your hand without being drawn, so the ability is not a draw trigger. Artifact creatures and enchantment creatures can fill either type slot, but one physical card cannot be selected twice. This list supports all nine relevant types, including kindred, battle, and planeswalker; that does not mean each trigger finds nine cards. Lifelink and vigilance stabilize combat, but do not protect her from removal.

Crib Swap

Three-mana creature exile that also gives an extra kindred option in Atraxa's reveal. Choose it as the kindred card and you may take a different instant too. Changeling and legendary are not additional card types for Atraxa. The opposing 1/1 token is a real drawback, but exile is useful against difficult creatures.

Baleful Strix

A cheap replacement card and flying deathtouch blocker, with both artifact and creature types for Atraxa. Blink it when the commander is unavailable and you need cards. Its small body is still useful under a later team pump.

Rhystic Study

Early card advantage while you build mana or hold up answers. It is one of the three Game Changers. Ask for the tax consistently, but do not assume opponents will decline to pay when you are threatening a finish.

Mystic Remora

A cheap early way to keep up with spell-heavy tables. Its cumulative upkeep can eventually slow your own development more than the cards are worth; letting it go is normal. Atraxa and Witness give you routes to rebuild resources afterward.

Sylvan Library

Early selection finds lands and missing colors. Atraxa's lifelink can later offset life spent on extra cards, but only if she actually connects. Fetchlands and land-search spells can shuffle away unwanted cards left on top. Atraxa putting cards in hand does not itself count as drawing for Library.

Esper Sentinel

Draw pressure on opposing noncreature spells, and an artifact-creature option in the reveal. It is a small blocker or eventual Finale attacker after serving its early role. Opponents can pay, so it is not guaranteed card advantage.

Tezzeret, Cruel Captain

Provides a planeswalker option for Atraxa and immediately untaps a mana creature or rock. His -3 finds Sol Ring or Esper Sentinel, not every artifact in the deck. Displacer Kitten can blink Tezzeret when you cast a noncreature spell, resetting loyalty and making him a new object; normal main-phase loyalty timing still applies.

Turn cheap spells into repeated entries
Ephemerate

One white mana protects and reuses a creature, then rebound can do it again next upkeep. Casting from hand exiles the spell as it resolves, so Witness cannot immediately return that first casting from the graveyard. A rebound casting is not from hand and normally goes to the graveyard afterward, allowing Witness to recover it then.

Essence Flux

Another one-mana immediate blink for your creatures. It can dodge targeted removal, refresh an entry trigger, or reset counters, but returning creatures are new objects and may be unable to attack or tap that turn. It cannot target a noncreature impending Overlord.

Ghostly Flicker

Blinks two artifacts, creatures, and/or lands you control. Reuse Atraxa plus another entry creature, save two targets from removal, or perform the mana loop described below. It cannot target a battle, planeswalker, or noncreature enchantment unless that permanent also has one of its allowed types. Tokens that leave do not return.

Soulherder

Repeats a creature entry at each of your end steps and grows as creatures are exiled from the battlefield. It can build value through Atraxa, Strix, Witness, or removal creatures. It does not blink itself and normally provides delayed end-step value rather than immediate protection from a spell.

Thassa, Deep-Dwelling

Another end-step blink engine that often survives creature wipes because blue devotion is below five. It functions as an enchantment regardless of whether it is currently a creature. Its tap ability can clear blockers or buy time and is a mana sink once the combo produces excess mana.

Displacer Kitten

Each noncreature spell you cast can blink one nonland permanent you control before that spell resolves. This turns ramp, interaction, and enchantments into extra Atraxa entries or other useful effects. It can blink an impending Overlord, unlike creature-only effects. It does not trigger from casting a creature spell and does not have the same target restriction as Ghostly Flicker.

Flickering Hound

The recent-printing counterpart for creature spells: casting a creature can blink another creature you already control. Flash creatures such as Solitude and Endurance let you trigger it on opposing turns. The creature spell is still on the stack, so it cannot be the creature blinked by that cast trigger.

Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd

Flash it in, then attack on a later turn to blink a nonland permanent until the next end step. It can reuse your own entry effects or temporarily remove a blocker or restrictive permanent. It does not grant itself haste, and its 2/2 body can die in combat even though an already-triggered delayed return still happens.

Touch the Spirit Realm

An enchantment option in Atraxa's reveal, temporary artifact/creature removal when cast, or a two-mana channel blink. Channel returns the target at the next end step rather than immediately. The channel activation is not a spell and does not trigger Displacer Kitten.

Phyrexian Metamorph

The final tuning addition replaces another slow end-step engine with a flexible artifact/creature copy. Copy a mana rock early or Archon, Drake, Craterhoof, or an opposing threat later. Copying your Atraxa gives another entry trigger, but the legend rule makes you keep only one; copying her does not create a second commander. Blinking Metamorph lets it choose a new copy among permanents already on the battlefield.

Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines

Doubles your relevant entry triggers while suppressing opponents' permanent-entry triggers. It can double Atraxa's refill, Drake's untaps, and Overlord's land creation. It does not double creature/noncreature CAST triggers from Hound or Kitten. It is strong disruption, not a total lock, and still needs protection from ordinary removal.

Interact without abandoning development
Swords to Plowshares

Along with Path to Exile, provides one-mana exile so you can answer a threat and still develop. Path may ramp the opponent; Swords gains them life. Neither answers an arbitrary noncreature permanent.

Anguished Unmaking

Exiles any opposing nonland permanent, including effects that stop your entry triggers or graveyard recursion. Assassin's Trophy can destroy a broader range, including a land, but gives a potential basic replacement. Save this broad coverage for obstacles that narrower creature answers cannot remove.

Swan Song

One-mana protection from an instant, sorcery, or enchantment spell. Dovin's Veto stops a noncreature spell uncounterably, and Flusterstorm helps win fights over instants and sorceries but is a payable tax. Keep colored mana available for the answer you actually hold.

Mana Drain

Broad countermagic with a later colorless mana refund. Use the refund for Atraxa's generic cost, a large Zenith, or Finale, but it does not replace the colored symbols in those costs. You still need to counter a spell worth answering rather than spending protection just to obtain mana.

Cyclonic Rift

Single-target tempo early or a seven-mana opposing-board reset late. Overload leaves your own creatures ready to attack and can open a combat finish. Returned cards are not permanently removed, so use the window rather than assuming opponents cannot rebuild.

Toxic Deluge

An emergency creature reset that can kill indestructible threats through toughness reduction. It also kills your dorks and entry creatures, and its life payment matters after fetches, shocks, or Reanimate. Choose the smallest useful X and retain a recovery line rather than clearing your own engine for little gain.

Solitude

Flash creature exile that can be cast by pitching a white card when you lack mana. Blinking it before the evoke sacrifice resolves keeps the returned new object and produces another entry trigger. Its normal mana value is still five for Atraxa's creature selection and reanimation life loss.

Endurance

Flash graveyard interaction that puts a player's graveyard on the bottom of their library. It can disrupt opposing recursion or save your own graveyard from exile, but moving your combo spell away also disrupts your own loop. Like Solitude, it can be blinked in response to its evoke sacrifice trigger.

Loran of the Third Path

Repeatable artifact/enchantment removal through blink, plus optional symmetrical card draw. Use it to remove the permanent preventing your engine from functioning. Giving an opponent a card is not free, particularly if you are preparing a combo turn.

Witch Enchanter // Witch-Blessed Meadow

A land option early or another artifact/enchantment removal creature later. In the library it counts as a creature, not a land, because only the front face applies. It remains a good blink target even when Atraxa is unavailable.

Sink into Stupor // Soporific Springs

A land option or temporary answer to an opposing spell or nonland permanent. Returning a spell to hand can handle an uncounterable spell, but the opponent can cast it again later. Its front face counts as an instant for Atraxa, not a land.

Protect and recover the engine
Veil of Summer

Cheap protection against blue and black interaction, with a card only if an opponent cast a spell of one of those colors that turn. It is not universal protection against white exile or untargeted sweepers. Hold it for the part of the turn that could otherwise stop your engine or finisher.

Heroic Intervention

Hexproof and indestructible protect your permanents from many targeted and destroy effects. Flawless Maneuver is free with Atraxa present but only makes creatures indestructible. Neither saves creatures from exile wipes, sacrifice, or sufficient toughness reduction.

Clever Concealment

Phases out chosen nonland permanents, with creatures helping pay through convoke even if summoning sick. It protects against many wipes that indestructible cannot answer. Phasing is not leaving or entering, so it does not retrigger Atraxa. It does not protect your lands or your life total.

Reanimate

Recover a dead entry creature or use a strong creature from an opposing graveyard for one black mana and life equal to its mana value. It cannot pull Atraxa from the command zone. Leaving her in the graveyard deliberately can avoid commander tax but exposes her to graveyard hate.

Animate Dead

A second efficient recovery effect with an enchantment card type. Blinking the recovered creature can detach it from the Aura's sacrifice dependency: the returned creature is a new object. Removing the Aura before you can do that threatens the creature it returned. Its -1/-0 also reduces Atraxa's combat damage while attached.

Necromancy

Three-mana recovery that can be cast outside sorcery timing. If cast that way it sacrifices itself at the next cleanup step, normally causing the returned creature to be sacrificed too. A blink before then can preserve the creature as a new object. This is an interaction to plan, not permanent instant-speed reanimation without conditions.

Eternal Witness

Returns any card from your graveyard to hand, enabling recovery of removal, blink, ramp, or a finisher. You still pay to cast the recovered card. It is the recursion half of the mana loop and a useful value creature even without that loop.

Win through combat or the assembled mana engine
Craterhoof Behemoth

Turns mana creatures and utility bodies into a trample attack, and has haste itself. Its entry pump affects creatures present when the trigger resolves and does not grant haste to the rest of your team. One or two bodies are usually not a table kill; count the actual board before committing eight mana.

→
Finale of Devastation → Craterhoof Behemoth

When you have a developed army and enough mana, Finale can find Hoof from the library or graveyard. X=8 costs ten mana and finds Hoof; X=10 costs twelve and also grants your team +10/+10 and haste, on top of Hoof's pump and trample. This is a genuine mana investment and combat line, not an early compact combo.

Archon of Cruelty

Each entry or attack pressures one opponent's board, hand, and life while drawing and gaining life for you. Repeat entries break a stalled game and the 6/6 flying body contributes to combat. It is the other expensive creature payoff, so avoid keeping it with a hand lacking acceleration.

++
Peregrine Drake + Eternal Witness + Ghostly Flicker

With both creatures on the battlefield and lands able to make more than three mana per iteration, cast Flicker on Drake and Witness for 2U. They return; Drake untaps up to five lands and Witness returns the now-resolved Flicker from the graveyard. With five ordinary one-mana lands, tap them for five, spend three on Flicker, and retain two each cycle. At least one land must provide unrestricted blue for the spell. Repeat for an arbitrarily large finite mana amount. To bank BLUE for Zenith and additional Flickers, use at least two unrestricted blue-producing lands among those five, or a land that produces extra blue. A single one-blue source can sustain the loop but spends that blue each iteration, so it does not by itself accumulate blue mana. Removal, graveyard exile, entry-trigger suppression, and counters can interrupt this; three cards is not permission to race into an early win. Use the assembled line as a turn-six-or-later Bracket 3 finish.

++
Atraxa, Grand Unifier + Eternal Witness + Ghostly Flicker

After banking mana with Drake, blink Atraxa and Witness together repeatedly, returning Flicker each time. Atraxa supplies selected cards until you find a finisher and protection; these are not draws. Casting Atraxa from the command zone first is possible using the banked mana if she was unavailable. This pair of targets CONSUMES mana and is not independently a mana-producing loop.

++→
Peregrine Drake + Eternal Witness + Ghostly Flicker → Blue Sun's Zenith

After generating enough blue mana, cast Zenith targeting an opponent with X greater than their library size. They lose when state-based actions are checked after the spell resolves, because they tried to draw from an empty library. Zenith shuffles into your library, not your graveyard, so Witness cannot immediately retrieve it. Repeat Atraxa entries to find it again and eliminate the next opponent. Draw-replacement effects and targeting restrictions can stop this route; remove them first or use combat.

++→
Peregrine Drake + Eternal Witness + Ghostly Flicker → Finale of Devastation

The alternate mana-loop finish makes X enormous, retrieves Hoof, and gives your existing creatures haste and a huge pump. Hoof supplies trample. Keep enough attackers to threaten the remaining opponents, and preserve an answer to a fog or relevant protection effect. Unlimited mana alone is not a win without a usable outlet.

+
Solitude + Ephemerate

Cast Solitude with evoke, then blink it while its sacrifice trigger is pending. The returned object stays, and both entries provide exile triggers. Endurance supports the same pattern for repeated graveyard interaction. This is a finite efficiency play that protects you while developing a body.

+
Overlord of the Hauntwoods + Displacer Kitten

Cast Overlord for impending, then cast a noncreature spell to have Kitten blink it. It returns as a creature with no impending counters and creates another Everywhere land. Phelia can also target the impending enchantment, but returns it at the end step. Creature-only blink cannot perform this shortcut while it is still a noncreature.

Choose tutors for the current bottleneck
→
Eladamri's Call → Faeburrow Elder

When you need a scalable mana creature and can wait through summoning sickness, Elder starts with two colors from itself and reaches four with Atraxa. Call costs two mana and puts the creature in hand; include that cost in the plan rather than counting it as direct ramp.

→
Eladamri's Call → Endurance

When a graveyard line threatens the game, find your flash answer. You still need 1GG or another green card to exile for evoke after resolving Call. This use of the tutor often matters more than collecting a combo piece while an opponent is about to win.

→
Eladamri's Call → Eternal Witness

When the crucial removal spell, blink spell, or finisher is already in your graveyard, Witness can recover it. In a developed late game, it can also complete the three-card engine when the other pieces are already available. Do not assume the tutor itself is protection from removal.

→
Tezzeret, Cruel Captain → Sol Ring

Use -3 when you need a mana rock more than an immediate untap. Tezzeret finds a card to hand, so Ring still costs one to cast. Esper Sentinel is the other relevant small artifact target when additional card pressure matters more than generic mana.

What to keep and how to sequence

Keep three useful lands, at least one early accelerator, and a route to either more mana or cards. For example: Windswept Heath, Polluted Delta, Command Tower, Birds of Paradise, Nature's Lore, Ephemerate, and Swan Song. Establish green first, then the other Atraxa colors, while leaving a real blue source untapped if Swan Song is your protection. Fetching a tapped triome is best when you have no use for that mana immediately.

The intended development is acceleration on turns one and two, another engine or interaction on turn three, and Atraxa around turns four or five when the actual hand supports it. These are targets, not guarantees. If casting her consumes every source and the table has obvious removal, consider waiting to retain a blink or counterspell. Do not keep seven-mana cards and assume the commander will somehow fix missed land drops.

Cavern of Souls usually names Phyrexian to cover Atraxa, Elesh Norn, and Metamorph, or Angel if that serves the immediate hand. Its colored mana cannot pay for your counterspells. Four basics are included for Invasion and opposing basic-search effects; preserve targets when possible. The two MDFCs offer additional land access but count as their spell front faces in Atraxa's reveal.

On Atraxa's entry, select for the next turn rather than taking the flashiest threat. A land, cheap answer, acceleration, and blink spell often outperform another expensive creature. Use multi-type cards to free other selection slots. Atraxa's normal maximum hand size still applies; discard can set up your recovery spells, but you cannot discard her directly from the command zone.

Keep the game plan flexible. Without Atraxa, Strix, the independent draw engines, Witness, removal creatures, and blink still generate value. Against graveyard hate, prioritize combat and card advantage instead of forcing the Witness loop. Against entry suppression, find broad removal before committing another expensive engine. Resolve the line that ends the game with protection rather than taking unnecessary extra value actions.
