Reach eight charge counters before counting land sacrifices as damage: Hearthhull's drain ability belongs to its 8+ station tier. At two counters it is already a powerful draw engine, but it is not yet a creature and does not drain anyone. Build reusable land drops and station creatures first, then convert a developed mana base into lethal sacrifice and landfall triggers.

Switching on the ship

Hearthhull, the Worldseed

At two charge counters, pay one, tap it, and sacrifice a land to draw two and gain an additional land play that turn. At eight it becomes a 6/7 flying, vigilant, hasty artifact creature and gains the trigger that makes EACH opponent lose two life whenever you sacrifice a land. Charge counters persist across turns; station is not a temporary crew effect.

Station only during your main phase with an empty stack. It taps another untapped creature as a cost, and can use a creature that entered this turn. That creature's power determines the counters added when the station ability resolves. You can station an already tapped Hearthhull, but reaching eight does not untap it. Before eight it can use its tap ability the turn it enters because it is not a creature; at eight its own haste handles summoning sickness.

Seedborn Muse

A Game Changer that untaps the ship, creatures, and lands during each opponent's untap step. That can mean another two cards on each opposing turn, but each activation costs a land. Hearthhull's additional land play never permits playing a land on an opponent's turn, so do not sacrifice faster than your next turn can rebuild.

Squandered Resources

A free land-sacrifice outlet that turns each land into one mana of a type it could produce. Tap a land for its normal mana first, then sacrifice it for the extra mana and triggers. The sacrifice ability is a mana ability, but Hearthhull's resulting drain triggers still use the stack. This is a finishing resource, not a reason to dismantle your early mana base.

Zuran Orb

Sacrifices lands for life without mana or a tap cost. Once Hearthhull is fully stationed, every land sacrificed gains you two life and makes each opponent lose two. It also saves life in emergencies while stocking the graveyard for a mass return.

Charging quickly with creatures that do useful work

Titania, Protector of Argoth

Returns a land on entry and makes a 5/3 Elemental whenever one of your lands goes from the battlefield to your graveyard. Return and crack a fetchland, then tap Titania and the new token to station for ten counters in total. New tokens can station immediately. Save any creature you still want to attack with instead of tapping it unnecessarily.

Lumra, Bellow of the Woods

Mills four and returns every land from your graveyard. Its power then equals your land count, often enough to station Hearthhull to eight by itself. Resolve the land return before deciding how many counters its station activation will supply. Its reach also gives the deck a substantial defensive body.

Mightform Harmonizer

Two land entries double a creature's power twice. An active six-power Hearthhull becomes 24 power, enough for a commander-damage kill if it connects. Alternatively, double a support creature to station the ship quickly. Below eight counters Hearthhull is not a creature and cannot be targeted by this landfall ability.

Soul of Windgrace

Its five power charges the ship efficiently, and its entry or attack can take a land from ANY graveyard. Discard spare lands for cards or to give Soul itself indestructible; that protection does not extend to Hearthhull. Spelunking turns the returned tapped land into immediately available mana.

Elvish Reclaimer

Starts as a cheap body and becomes three power once three land cards are in your graveyard. Its two-mana tap-and-sacrifice ability finds any land, replacing the sacrificed land rather than ramping your land count. Stationing it now means giving up its own tap activation until it untaps.

Hexing Squelcher

A two-power body that switches on Hearthhull's first tier while making your spells uncounterable. Its life-payment ward on other creatures adds friction to removal, but does not stop it. Hearthhull only receives that creature protection once it is actually a creature.

Making sacrifices reusable

Life from the Loam

Returns up to three lands to hand so extra land plays keep working. You can replace one of Hearthhull's two draws with dredge three and draw the other normally. Recovering Loam is not drawing a card, and dredging consumes library cards, so balance recursion against finding new interaction.

Ramunap Excavator

Replays fetchlands from the graveyard using your available land plays. Playing and cracking one gives two landfall events and one sacrifice trigger each time. It does not grant an extra land play by itself.

Crucible of Worlds

The noncreature version of the land-replay engine, surviving your creature sweepers. Redundant replay effects do not grant additional land drops; pair them with Hearthhull, Exploration, Azusa, or Dryad rather than deploying all of them without a reason.

Icetill Explorer

Combines graveyard land access, an extra land play, and landfall mill in one creature. Its two power immediately enables the ship's draw tier. Landfall mill is mandatory, so watch library size during very large return turns, especially with Ancient Greenwarden.

Mole Man, Moloid Master

Replays graveyard lands while every land entry makes another creature to station, block, or eventually attack. The Moloids' attack mill is optional. One-power bodies are inefficient individually, but a fetchland produces two and repeated land returns supply a whole crew.

Ancient Greenwarden

Replays lands and doubles triggered abilities caused by lands entering, including your landfall engines and Field of the Dead. It does NOT double Hearthhull's sacrifice trigger. It also doubles Lotus Field's entry trigger, requiring two separate sacrifices of two lands, so use that combination deliberately.

Aftermath Analyst

Mills lands early, then sacrifices itself for four mana to return all lands in your graveyard tapped. Unlike a sorcery, the activation can be used at instant speed. Sacrificing Analyst is part of the cost; ordinary creature removal cannot undo an activation already on the stack, although graveyard exile can ruin its payoff.

Splendid Reclamation

A four-mana recovery spell that turns a graveyard of fetchlands and sacrificed lands into a large landfall burst. Reserve the mana before sacrificing lands to Zuran Orb, and retain protection if opponents can counter the spell or exile your graveyard.

Six

Recovers nonland permanent engines by letting you cast them from the graveyard with retrace during your turn. Discarding a land for retrace stocks the land engine, but is not sacrificing a land and does not trigger Hearthhull. Six does not recur instant or sorcery cards.

Converting land access into mana and repeated triggers

Exploration

One mana for an extra land play is excellent with Loam, Crucible, and Hearthhull's draw. It does nothing without another land available, so a hand of extra-land effects but no draw or recursion is a poor keep.

Azusa, Lost but Seeking

Supplies two additional land plays each turn, making a replayable fetchland especially productive. Her one power contributes slowly to station; prefer preserving her as an engine rather than exposing her in combat.

Dryad of the Ilysian Grove

Adds a land play, fixes all your lands to produce any of your colours, and turns every land into a Mountain for Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle. The added types preserve existing names and abilities, so Field of the Dead still counts differently named lands normally.

Oracle of Mul Daya

Adds a land play and lets you play lands from the top of your library. This preserves cards in hand and helps clear the top before a Hearthhull draw. Use fetchland shuffles when the revealed top card is unhelpful, remembering opponents can see that information too.

Lotus Cobra

Turns landfall into mana to pay for Hearthhull activations, recursion, and protection. A fetchland entering and finding another land generates two triggers. Floating mana disappears at step and phase boundaries, so plan a main-phase return when you want to cast sorceries afterward.

Nissa, Resurgent Animist

Makes mana on landfall and, on the second resolution of her ability each turn, finds an Elf or Elemental. This list has useful hits ranging from Elvish Reclaimer and Aftermath Analyst to Titania, Lumra, and Ancient Greenwarden. It is not a tutor for whichever one you want.

Tireless Provisioner

Usually choose Treasure to bank landfall mana through combat and into later turns. Choose Food when life is the constraint. Its three power also makes useful station progress, and sacrificing Treasure triggers Korvold and Mayhem Devil even though it does not trigger Hearthhull.

Spelunking

Replaces itself, can put a land from hand onto the battlefield, and makes your lands enter untapped. That last effect turns Splendid Reclamation, Lumra, and Scapeshift into immediate mana instead of a turn of waiting.

Horizon Explorer

Another way to have lands enter untapped, attached to a two-power station body. Attacking a player also makes a Lander; its activation searches only for a basic land, and sacrificing the Lander artifact itself is not a land sacrifice.

Amulet of Vigor

The cheap, Saga-searchable way to untap lands returned tapped. Unlike Spelunking, it uses a trigger that opponents can respond to. If Spelunking already makes a land enter untapped, Amulet does not trigger for that land.

Harrow

Sacrifices a land as an additional casting cost and finds two basic lands untapped. A counterspell costs you both the spell and the sacrificed land, so be careful into open blue mana. It supplies a sacrifice trigger and two landfall entries when it resolves.

Entish Restoration

Sacrifices its land during resolution, so a countered spell does not cost you that land. It finds two basics, or three if you control a creature with power at least four, such as a fully stationed Hearthhull or Soul of Windgrace. The six differently named basics support these searches while contributing to Field of the Dead; count targets remaining before casting repeated copies over a game.

How the deck actually kills a table

Scapeshift

Sacrifice a developed set of lands, drain opponents with an active Hearthhull, then replace the lands with your strongest land package. Sacrificing ten lands drains each opponent for twenty before accounting for other payoffs. Fetch Field of the Dead and differently named lands for a board, or Valakut with Dryad already active for damage. The sacrifice happens during resolution, so countering Scapeshift does not make you lose those lands.

Field of the Dead

A Game Changer and a win engine that does not need your commander. When seven or more differently named lands enter together with Field among them, it triggers for each entering land if the name threshold is met. Zombies defend you, station the ship, and attack in later turns. Ancient Greenwarden doubles the eligible triggers.

Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle

With Dryad active, every land is a Mountain. Six or more lands entering together can each trigger Valakut, including Valakut itself, because each sees at least five other Mountains. This damage can remove blockers or finish players. The five-other-Mountains condition must still hold when a trigger resolves; losing Dryad can switch it off.

Moraug, Fury of Akoum

Turns main-phase landfall into extra combats without taking extra turns. Usually play your big land-return spell in the SECOND main phase, after taking the normal combat, then untap your creatures at the start of each additional combat. If you trigger it in the first main phase, the extra combats happen before normal combat, and normal combat does not itself untap your team. New tokens still need haste or a later turn to attack.

Scute Swarm

Once six lands are in play, successive land entries grow a large copy army. Fetchlands and mass returns accelerate that growth, although lands entering simultaneously do not let newly created copies see earlier entries. These bodies station immediately but cannot attack immediately without haste.

Tannuk, Memorial Ensign

Each landfall deals one to EVERY opponent, independently of Hearthhull's station count. The second resolution of its landfall ability each turn also draws a card, once per turn. Replayed fetchlands make this an efficient source of both pressure and cards.

Mayhem Devil

Turns sacrifices by ANY player into targeted pings. Use these to remove small disruptive creatures, finish a planeswalker, or add damage to a sacrifice turn. Hearthhull hits every opponent, while each Devil trigger only damages its chosen target.

Worldsoul's Rage

Converts spare mana into targeted damage and up to X lands returned from hand and/or graveyard. It is both interaction and a late payoff for Squandered Resources or landfall mana. If its only damage target becomes illegal before resolution, the whole spell fails to resolve and you do not get the lands.

A concrete late-game kill is a fully stationed Hearthhull, Zuran Orb, Splendid Reclamation in hand, and ten lands. Float at least four mana, sacrifice ten lands to Orb to make every opponent lose twenty life, then cast Reclamation. After the ten lands return, sacrifice them again for another twenty. The returned lands can be tapped. This is a finite line requiring an established board, not an infinite loop; an opponent can still interrupt the drain triggers, commander, recursion, or graveyard access.

You gain forty life from the twenty Orb activations in that example. If additional creatures such as Korvold or The Gitrog Monster are present, their draw triggers must also be handled; do not accidentally draw past the end of your library. With Lotus Field involved, carefully account for its additional sacrifice triggers rather than assuming all returned lands stay in play.

Drawing through removal without relying only on Hearthhull

Korvold, Fae-Cursed King

Draws and grows from every permanent you sacrifice, including lands, Treasure, and Aftermath Analyst. Multiple lands sacrificed simultaneously generate separate Korvold triggers. The sacrifice demanded by its entry also starts the engine immediately, often leaving a five-power station body.

The Gitrog Monster

Gives an extra land play and draws whenever one or more land cards enter your graveyard from anywhere. A fetchland sacrifice and Icetill's mill are separate events, but sacrificing ten lands simultaneously only gives one Gitrog draw trigger. Its upkeep sacrifice is another cost you must budget for.

Sylvan Library

Helps find land access or protection while the commander is still being assembled. Paying life to keep extra cards is more comfortable when Zuran Orb is available, but do not pay away the life needed to survive combat or cast Toxic Deluge.

Valakut Exploration

Landfall provides temporary access to cards, and unused exiled cards move to the graveyard at your end step while the enchantment damages each opponent for their number. You must pay costs and obey normal timing to play them. A large land-return turn can therefore provide both selection and table-wide damage without Hearthhull.

Protecting the engine and answering the table

Sylvan Safekeeper

Sacrifices a land to give a creature shroud. It protects Hearthhull only at eight counters, when the ship is a creature. Repeated activations can also supply lethal sacrifice triggers, but shroud prevents your own targeted spells and abilities from targeting the protected creature afterward.

Tamiyo's Safekeeping

Protects ANY one permanent with hexproof and indestructible, including Hearthhull before it becomes a creature or a threatened recursion enchantment. It does not save a permanent from sacrifices or toughness reduction.

Heroic Intervention

Protects your developed board against many sweepers while preserving your landfall engines. It does not stop graveyard exile, an untargeted exile sweeper, or effects that force sacrifice. Keep it available before investing everything in a mass-return turn.

Deflecting Swat

Works for free while you control Hearthhull even when the ship is not a creature. Redirect a targeted removal spell or graveyard-hate ability where its legal targets allow. It cannot solve an untargeted wipe or an ability with no targets.

Veil of Summer

Protects a decisive spell from being countered that turn and gives your permanents hexproof from blue and black. It draws only if an opponent has cast a blue or black spell that turn. It does not counter abilities or protect against every colour of interaction.

Tear Asunder

Exiles an artifact or enchantment cheaply, or any nonland permanent when kicked. Graveyard-locking enchantments are priority targets because they can disable several engines at once. Keep enough mana to remove the hate before committing a recovery spell.

Assassin's Trophy

Answers a dangerous permanent at instant speed, while Abrupt Decay handles cheaper nonland threats without being counterable. Beast Within provides another broad answer, and Deadly Rollick exiles a creature for free while Hearthhull is present. These are tools to preserve your engine or stop a win, not excuses to trade away your whole hand.

Toxic Deluge

A low-cost reset before your slower engines take over. Below eight counters Hearthhull is not a creature and is untouched. At eight, a sufficiently large negative toughness effect kills it through indestructible. Your Dryad Arbor is always a creature and is exposed to creature wipes.

Blasphemous Act

A larger creature-board reset that also leaves an unstationed or partially stationed noncreature Hearthhull intact. Once the commander becomes a creature, protect it or accept rebuilding. It can also kill your own station crew and valuable draw engines, so do not cast it simply because it costs one mana.

Vandalblast

One-sided artifact removal when overloaded, preserving Hearthhull, Crucible, Amulet, and your other artifacts. The single-target mode efficiently removes an early problem piece; save overload for a board where it earns its cost.

Tutor choices and land sequencing

Crop Rotation

The third Game Changer combines a sacrifice trigger with instant-speed access to any land. Find Bojuka Bog against a graveyard threat, Field of the Dead for an independent board, or a needed colour source when development is the bottleneck. It sacrifices a land as an additional cost, so a counterspell leaves you down the land.

Green Sun's Zenith

Find Dryad Arbor with X equal to zero for early acceleration; Arbor has summoning sickness and cannot tap for mana immediately. It CAN station another permanent immediately. Later, find Icetill Explorer for recursion and land plays, Titania for station power, or Lumra for mass recovery. Zenith puts the creature onto the battlefield without casting it.

Urza's Saga

Makes Construct tokens that can station or defend, then searches for Sol Ring, Amulet of Vigor, or Zuran Orb. It cannot find Arcane Signet. The Saga sacrifices itself after its final chapter, triggering a fully stationed Hearthhull and becoming a useful land to replay from the graveyard.

Boseiju, Who Endures

Channel answers artifacts, enchantments, or nonbasic lands while your land recursion can recover it later. Discarding Boseiju to channel is not a sacrifice and does not trigger Hearthhull. Takenuma, Abandoned Mire similarly provides a land-slot way to recover a creature from your graveyard.

Bojuka Bog

Search it onto the battlefield with Crop Rotation or Elvish Reclaimer when a graveyard becomes dangerous. Returning it from your own graveyard repeats its exile trigger. Holding it in hand gives no instant-speed graveyard interaction by itself, because normal land-play timing still applies.

Lotus Field

Provides three mana from one land and puts two others into the graveyard for later recovery. Tap the lands you intend to sacrifice before its entry trigger resolves. Spelunking or Amulet makes Field available immediately, but its sacrifices are mandatory even if your graveyard engine is removed. Ancient Greenwarden doubles that entry trigger, not the mana ability.

Shifting Woodland can copy a useful permanent card in your graveyard once you have delirium. Becoming a copy does not cause entry triggers, so copying Lumra does not return your lands. Copying Aftermath Analyst gives you its sacrifice activation; copying a replay engine can restore land access for the turn. Check the mana needed after paying for the copy ability.

What to keep in an opening hand and how to sequence

Keep three or four lands, early green access, acceleration or extra land plays, and a creature or draw engine that gets the ship working. A hand with only rocks and expensive payoffs can cast Hearthhull but cannot station it. A hand of multiple recursion effects without lands to play or sacrifice is similarly slow.

An example keep is Verdant Catacombs, Command Tower, Forest, Nature's Lore, Lotus Cobra, Icetill Explorer, and Tamiyo's Safekeeping. Nature's Lore can fetch Taiga or Bayou to establish a missing colour; the original duals carry the Forest type and can enter untapped. Decide whether Cobra's explosive line or guaranteed land ramp better matches the table's removal.

The opening objective is mana plus a two-power creature, followed by Hearthhull's draw engine. Get to eight counters when the drain or combat damage matters, but recognize that remaining a noncreature artifact can protect the ship through your own creature sweeper. You choose when to add more counters; there is no requirement to finish stationing immediately.

Once fully charged, attack with vigilance before tapping Hearthhull for cards if the attack is safe. Sequence replayed fetchlands around Nissa and Tannuk's second-resolution bonuses, and save land-return spells for turns with a real payoff. Fetch duals for reliable early colours; fetch basics when that preserves typed dual targets for later or supports your remaining basic-search spells.

Against graveyard hate, preserve lands in hand and use draw, Field of the Dead, Scute Swarm, or ordinary landfall to keep developing. Remove the hate before sacrificing your mana base. Against removal-heavy tables, keep more than one recovery route and avoid exposing every creature engine to the same wipe.

The three Game Changers are Crop Rotation, Field of the Dead, and Seedborn Muse. The intended finishes are finite land-sacrifice bursts, landfall damage, and combat from a developed board. There is no planned infinite combo, extra-turn chain, or opposing mass land denial. The build is designed to accumulate powerful resources through Bracket 3's normal game length and then close decisively.
