Every tax in this deck is symmetrical on paper and one-sided in practice, because Grand Arbiter refunds you. Sphere of Resistance costs everyone {1} more — and gives you {1} back on every white or blue spell you cast. Stack three or four of those effects and the table simply stops being able to cast anything, while your own hand gets cheaper.

The commander and the refund

Grand Arbiter Augustin IV

your white spells and your blue spells each cost {1} less; theirs all cost {1} more. Nearly every card in the deck is white or blue, so the discount applies to almost everything you do.

Sphere of Resistance

everything costs {1} more. With Arbiter out, your white and blue spells are back to full price and theirs are up two.

Thalia, Guardian of Thraben

the same tax aimed at noncreature spells, on a first-striking body.

Lodestone Golem

nonartifact spells cost {1} more, and it is a 5/3 that actually attacks.

God-Pharaoh's Statue

two more on every spell they cast, plus a drain each turn — it is a clock and a lock in one card.

Sapphire Medallion

another {1} off your blue spells, stacking with Arbiter.

Pearl Medallion

and the white half. With Arbiter plus a Medallion, two-mana spells are free.

Aura of Silence

their artifacts and enchantments cost two more, and it eats one on the way out.

Spelltithe Enforcer

they pay {1} per spell or lose a permanent. With the taxes above, the {1} is not always available.

One spell per turn

The tax package makes each spell expensive; this package makes it their ONLY spell. Together they end games.

Rule of Law

one spell per turn, each. The enchantment version, hardest to remove.

Archon of Emeria

the same, on a 2/3 flier, plus their nonbasics enter tapped.

Eidolon of Rhetoric

a third copy on a 1/4 that blocks well.

Ethersworn Canonist

one nonartifact spell each, which is strictly better for you than for a table not built on artifacts.

Deafening Silence

one noncreature spell each, for one mana, and it never affects your creature deployment.

Sanctum Prelate

name the number the table is built around — usually two or three — and those spells simply cannot be cast at all.

Lavinia, Azorius Renegade

they cannot cast noncreature spells above their land count, and anything cast for free is countered outright. She blanks Force of Will, ritual-fuelled combo turns and cascade cheats.

Teferi, Time Raveler

they can only act at sorcery speed. No instant-speed removal, no counterspells on your turn.

Locking their one spell

Jin-Gitaxias, Progress Tyrant

counters the first artifact, instant or sorcery each opponent casts each turn AND copies yours. Once a one-spell-per-turn effect is out, the one spell they get is the one Jin-Gitaxias counters.

Grand Abolisher

they cannot cast anything at all during your turn.

Myrel, Shield of Argive

the same lockout on a body that also builds an army every attack.

Silence

one mana for a whole turn of protection. Cast it before the combo turn or before the wrath.

Counterspell

the clean answer that never rots in hand.

Mana Drain

counter, and the floating mana deploys two more prison pieces.

Dovin's Veto

uncounterable, for the counter war you cannot afford to lose.

Force of Negation

free on their turn, which matters when you have tapped out to deploy.

Swan Song

one mana, and a 2/2 Bird is nothing against Ghostly Prison.

The wall

Ghostly Prison

they pay {2} per attacker or go hit somebody else. In a pod, that is usually enough on its own.

Blind Obedience

their creatures and artifacts arrive tapped, so no surprise blocker and no haste kill, and extort drains them every time you cast.

Linvala, Keeper of Silence

no creature activated abilities for them. Mana dorks, sac outlets and tutor creatures all stop.

Grafdigger's Cage

nothing comes back from graveyards or libraries.

Rest in Peace

the full version, and this deck never uses its own graveyard.

Aven Interrupter

flash, exiles a spell off the stack, and taxes everything they try to cast from exile or graveyards afterwards.

Aven Mindcensor

flash it onto the fetchland or the tutor.

Archivist of Oghma

a card and a life every time anybody searches.

Removal

Swords to Plowshares

one mana, exile, unconditional.

Generous Gift

any permanent, at instant speed.

March of Otherworldly Light

scalable exile, and the white cards you pitch make it cheap on a locked-out turn.

Supreme Verdict

uncounterable, and it usually leaves your board — enchantments, artifacts, planeswalkers — untouched.

Hullbreaker Horror

uncounterable, flash, and every spell you cast afterwards bounces a permanent or an entire spell off the stack. Under a one-spell-per-turn lock this is unbeatable.

How you actually win

Approach of the Second Sun

the natural finisher for a deck that stops the game. Six mana with Arbiter out, gain seven life, then cast it again seven cards later and win. Note it must be cast FROM YOUR HAND both times.

Luminarch Ascension

nobody can profitably attack you through Ghostly Prison, so the quest counters accumulate for free. Then it makes a 4/4 flier every turn.

Myrel, Shield of Argive

her attack trigger doubles your Soldier count every combat. Two attacks after she lands, the board is lethal.

Shark Typhoon

a flier for every noncreature spell, or cycled at instant speed for an immediate one.

Rise and Shine

overloaded, every mana rock you control becomes a 4/4. This deck plays a lot of rocks.

Cyberdrive Awakener

animates every noncreature artifact as a 4/4 AND gives them all flying. Alongside Rise and Shine it is a lethal alpha strike from a board of Signets.

Akroma's Will

with your commander out, take both halves — the finisher for any of the above.

Copying the lock

Sakashima of a Thousand Faces

copy Grand Arbiter. The legend rule does not apply to your permanents, so you keep both — their spells now cost {2} more and yours {2} less.

Spark Double

the same trick, and it can copy Jin-Gitaxias or Myrel instead.

Multiversal Recruitment

a nonlegendary copy of anything you control, twice with flashback.

Quantum Misalignment

another copy, and rebound makes it two.

Mana and cards

Rhystic Study

in a deck taxing every spell they cast, the extra {1} is rarely available.

Mystic Remora

the first four turns it is the best card in the deck.

Smothering Tithe

Treasures that pay for the taxes you have imposed on yourself.

Esper Sentinel

taxes their first noncreature spell each turn on turn one.

Sol Ring

the fastest possible start toward a turn-three Arbiter.

Lotus Petal

fixes and accelerates, and it is free under Sphere of Resistance effects.

Mind Stone

ramp early, a card later.

What to keep in an opening hand

Keep a rock and a tax piece. Sol Ring or a Signet into Thalia, Deafening Silence or Sphere of Resistance on turn two is a far better start than a hand with Approach of the Second Sun in it.

Grand Arbiter Augustin IV

he is the discount, so land him before you commit to a hand of expensive prison pieces. Four mana on turn three or four, then two pieces a turn afterwards.

Deploy taxes before locks. Sphere of Resistance and Thalia hurt everyone a little; Rule of Law and Sanctum Prelate hurt them a lot — and if you drop the hard lock first, they untap and remove it with the mana the taxes would have eaten.

Approach of the Second Sun

only start it when the board is actually locked. Seven cards is several turns, and casting the first copy into an open table just tells them what you are doing.
