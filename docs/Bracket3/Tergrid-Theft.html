Plain text ↗ On GitHub ↗

Resolve Tergrid before your biggest discard or sacrifice effect, and protect her until that effect resolves. Casting every discard spell early leaves opponents empty-handed before you can steal anything. Develop mana and an independent draw engine first; then turn one well-timed spell into a board and use that advantage to finish.

The plan and its limits

This is a Bracket 3 theft deck with a secondary artifact combo and drain finish. The three Game Changers are Tergrid, God of Fright, Bolas's Citadel, and The One Ring. The list has no intentional mass land denial, extra-turn chain, or cheap two-card infinite. Fast development supports a turn-six-or-later game plan; the three-card finish is a backup to normal play, not an instruction to race the bracket.

Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern

Steals nontoken permanents opponents sacrifice and permanent cards they discard, including discarded lands. Destroying, milling, or exiling a card does not qualify. Opponents choose their sacrifices and discards unless an effect says otherwise; they can discard instants and sorceries to deny you a permanent. Your own sacrifices do not trigger her, even when you sacrifice a stolen card.

+
Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern + Dark Deal

Resolve the discard and redraw fully, then put the theft triggers on the stack. You can take every qualifying discarded permanent still in its graveyard, but opponents have fresh cards with which to respond. Tergrid must be present when the discard happens; once triggered, removing her does not erase her existing triggers.

+
Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern + Rankle's Prank

Choose discard and sacrifice when both are profitable. Discard happens before the creature sacrifices, and the theft triggers wait until the entire spell finishes. Sacrificing Tergrid with the creatures does not prevent her from seeing that simultaneous sacrifice event. The life-loss mode is optional and can be dangerous when you are paying life elsewhere.

+
Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern + Blasphemous Edict

A recovery swing against developed creature boards: sacrifice your creatures and then collect qualifying opposing creatures. Tergrid sees the simultaneous sacrifices even if she is among them. Thirteen creatures anywhere on the battlefield unlock the one-black alternative cost; you do not need thirteen yourself. Graveyard replacement effects can stop the theft entirely.

Momentum Breaker

Two mana can remove a creature or Vehicle from each opponent without sacrificing your own commander. An opponent who cannot sacrifice discards instead. Its later life-gain sacrifice can clear an enchantment you no longer need, and it supplies bargain material for Beseech the Mirror.

Delirium Skeins

A three-mana theft burst after Tergrid lands, but it also discards three of your cards. Spend important ramp first and preserve protection if possible. With no commander or draw payoff, emptying your own hand can leave you just as stranded as the table.

Get ahead on mana without abandoning the game plan
Sol Ring

The strongest opening acceleration. Spend its colorless mana on rocks and the generic part of Tergrid's cost; you still need two black mana to cast her.

+
Arcane Signet + The Soul Stone

Reliable black-producing two-mana rocks help cast Tergrid and the heavy-black finishers. The Soul Stone survives destruction; its expensive harness ability is a late recovery option, not something to prioritize over developing the board.

+
Mind Stone + Thought Vessel

Untapped two-mana acceleration supports a fourth-turn commander off four lands. Mind Stone becomes a card later; Thought Vessel lets a large Geth's Grimoire turn preserve all its cards through cleanup.

+
Fellwar Stone + Liquimetal Torque

More two-mana acceleration. Fellwar Stone only produces black if an opposing land could, while Torque normally supplies colorless mana. Do not count either as guaranteed black when evaluating an opening hand.

Jet Medallion

Discounts the generic portion of black spells, including Tergrid's command-zone tax, but produces no mana and cannot reduce black pips. It is especially useful for casting a black draw spell and a black edict in the same turn. It does not reduce life paid to Bolas's Citadel.

Dark Ritual

Converts one black mana into three. Use the burst for Tergrid plus a cheap follow-up or protection, rather than putting an undefended commander into three opponents' removal. It is temporary acceleration and should not replace land drops in a keep decision.

++
Cabal Coffers + Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth + Crypt Ghast

The big-mana engine. Urborg makes all your lands Swamps, Coffers counts them, and Ghast adds one black whenever you tap a Swamp for mana. Ghast adds just one extra black to a Coffers activation, not a second copy of its entire output. Without Urborg, the 26 basic Swamps still support both cards.

+
Bubbling Muck + Torment of Hailfire

Turn a developed Swamp count into a finishing spell. Muck adds one black for each Swamp tapped for mana this turn; it does not double Coffers' full output. It is symmetrical, and Urborg also makes opponents' lands Swamps, so expect them to have extra mana for responses.

Draw enough to function without the commander
+
Waste Not + Dark Deal

Discarded lands give mana, creatures give Zombies, and noncreature nonland cards give draws. These rewards work without Tergrid and can coexist with her theft triggers. All those triggers resolve after Dark Deal finishes; spend the resulting mana in the same step or phase.

+
Geth's Grimoire + Dark Deal

Each card an opponent discards creates an optional draw trigger. A large multiplayer discard becomes a refill, but do not draw your entire library. Grimoire does nothing when opponents have no cards left to discard, so do not rely on it as your only draw engine.

+
The One Ring + Sheoldred, the Apocalypse

Ring supplies increasing cards, while Sheoldred gains two life for each card you draw. Cast Ring before a vulnerable setup turn to gain protection until your next turn. That protection does not protect your permanents or prevent Ring's upkeep life loss. If its burden becomes dangerous, Braids or Deadly Dispute can sacrifice it despite indestructible.

Black Market Connections

Usually choose a Treasure and a card for three life. Add the creature only when you need a blocker, sacrifice material, or pressure; all three modes cost six life every turn. This engine works even when Tergrid is unavailable and opponents are empty-handed.

+
Tinybones, Trinket Thief + Oppression

Opponents casting during different turns can enable Tinybones at several end steps per round. The draw checks whether an opponent actually discarded that turn, not merely whether a discard instruction occurred. Oppression also taxes your own hand and never stops someone casting with an empty hand; it is not a hard lock.

Syphon Mind

One-sided discard plus up to three cards in a four-player game. You draw only for cards actually discarded, so cast it while opponents still have hands. Night's Whisper is the cheaper unconditional refill when they do not.

+
Braids, Arisen Nightmare + Virus Beetle

Cash in the spent Beetle at your end step. Because it is both an artifact and a creature, opponents can sacrifice either type to match, or decline and give you a card while losing two life. Sacrifice an ordinary creature instead when you want to narrow their choices. Braids does not force a sacrifice.

Aclazotz, Deepest Betrayal // Temple of the Dead

Converts attacks into discard while opponents have cards, and into draws when they do not. Flying and lifelink make it a relevant independent attacker and life engine. Its death trigger returns it as a tapped land; it does not immediately return as a creature.

Disrupt the threats that matter
+
Sheoldred's Edict + Soul Shatter

These answer multiple opponents without targeting their creatures. Edict's nontoken mode gets past token fodder; Soul Shatter demands a creature or planeswalker with the greatest mana value. Neither lets you choose an opposing creature when several options qualify.

Vona's Hunger

Before ascend, it is an instant-speed one-creature edict for each opponent. With ten permanents, it instead takes half each opposing creature board, rounded up. Rocks, lands, and stolen permanents make the city's blessing realistic without a token subtheme.

Flare of Malice

Lets you answer the biggest opposing creatures or planeswalkers while tapped out, but its alternative cost requires a nontoken black creature. The colorless token from Black Market Connections cannot pay it. Sacrificing Tergrid as the cost removes her before opponents sacrifice, so you get no theft triggers from that Flare.

+
Accursed Marauder + Plaguecrafter

Usually sacrifice the entering creature to its own trigger and preserve Tergrid. Marauder demands nontoken creatures; Plaguecrafter can take planeswalkers and forces a discard only when a player cannot sacrifice. Both remain useful interaction without the commander.

+
Feed the Swarm + Withering Torment

Direct enchantment answers prevent a single graveyard replacement or ability-removing enchantment from shutting off the plan. Feed is cheaper but sorcery-speed and its life loss scales with the target's mana value. Torment can clear an enchantment at instant speed before your theft spell resolves.

Blast Zone

A land-slot answer to troublesome artifacts and other nonland permanents of a chosen mana value. Adding X counters costs twice X mana and taps the land, so adding counters and detonating normally require separate turns. It hits your matching permanents too; at one counter that includes Sol Ring and Sensei's Divining Top.

Soul-Guide Lantern

The entry trigger removes one important graveyard card; the sacrifice ability can stop an opposing graveyard combo. Do not exile an opponent's graveyard while your own Tergrid triggers are waiting to return cards from it. When graveyard interaction is unnecessary, cash it in for a card or bargain material.

+
Snuff Out + Deadly Rollick

Mana-free interaction helps you commit to Tergrid without surrendering all responses. Snuff Out needs a Swamp, costs four life, and cannot kill black creatures. Rollick needs a commander under your control and exiles, so it does not feed Tergrid.

Toxic Deluge

The emergency reset against tokens or creatures the edicts cannot isolate. Tergrid normally survives X of four or less, but other creatures you control may not. Destruction protection cannot prevent toughness reduction, and destroying or killing opposing creatures this way does not trigger theft.

Keep the engine alive and recover it
Lightning Greaves

Protects Tergrid against targeted interaction and can give a stolen attacker haste later. Shroud also stops your own targeted protection and Aura spells. Move Greaves away before targeting Tergrid, and remember opponents can respond to an equip activation. Haste does not let you attack with a whole stolen army just by moving Greaves among them before combat.

+
Kaya's Ghostform + Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern

Recovers Tergrid after death or exile, provided you leave the card in that zone rather than moving it to the command zone. It does not answer bounce. Attach it before equipping Greaves. Returning her is a new battlefield object, and it cannot restore theft triggers missed while she was absent.

+
Malakir Rebirth // Malakir Mire + Not Dead After All

One-black protection against a coming death, or a way to reuse Gray Merchant. Both return under the owner's control, so using them on an opponent-owned stolen creature gives that creature back to its owner. Neither saves a creature from exile or bounce. Malakir Rebirth can instead be your tapped land drop.

Imp's Mischief

Redirects a spell with exactly one target when another legal target exists. It cannot change an ability or a multi-target spell. Against an ordinary counterspell, you can redirect it to the resolving Mischief; the counterspell will later have an illegal target. Restrictions on the counterspell still matter, and you lose life equal to the redirected spell's mana value.

+
Reanimate + Animate Dead

Recover a removed commander left in the graveyard, reuse Gray Merchant, or take the best creature discarded before Tergrid arrived. These do not recover a commander already moved to the command zone. Reanimate spends life; removing Animate Dead later normally makes you sacrifice its creature.

+
Agadeem's Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt + Takenuma, Abandoned Mire

Recovery in land slots. Awakening can return multiple creatures only if their mana values are different, with each at most X. Takenuma mills three and returns a creature or planeswalker to hand, not directly to the battlefield. Use either as a land when otherwise missing an early land drop.

How the deck actually wins
+
Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern + Lightning Greaves

The default win is a stolen board attacking after opponents lose blockers and resources. Most stolen creatures have summoning sickness; Greaves accelerates one attacker at a time. Keep enough removal for a blocker or recovery creature instead of treating your first large theft as a guaranteed win.

++
Bolas's Citadel + Sensei's Divining Top + Aetherflux Reservoir

With all three on the battlefield, tap Top to draw a card and put Top on your library. Cast Top from the library by paying one life through Citadel. Reservoir triggers on that cast and gains life equal to the number of spells you have cast this turn. Let the trigger and Top resolve, then repeat. Each cycle draws one card and increases subsequent life gains, so this is library-bounded, not an infinite loop. It requires no mana per cycle once assembled and works without Tergrid.

For the Reservoir finish, start with more than one life, enough cards remaining to draw, and no effect preventing life gain or further casts. If the first Top recast is your first spell of the turn, n cycles net n(n-1)/2 life. Starting at 40, sixteen cycles reach 160 before other effects, enough to pay 50 three times and remain at 10. Stop with at least one library card left and enough life to stay above zero after your intended payments. Fifty damage is not lethal to every possible opponent; check life totals, protection, and damage prevention. An opponent can respond at every normal priority window. Oppression adds a discard to each cast, but does not prevent the loop when your hand becomes empty.

Bolas's Citadel

An independent card-advantage engine even without the combo. Lands on top still obey normal land-play limits; Top's draw ability can clear a land after your land drop. X must be zero when casting Torment of Hailfire through Citadel's alternative cost, so cast the finisher from hand using mana. The ten-permanent sacrifice ability can finish low-life opponents, including by sacrificing stolen nonlands, but your sacrifices do not trigger Tergrid.

+
Gray Merchant of Asphodel + Saw in Half

A finite burst finish with life recovery. Saw destroys your Merchant and creates two smaller copies that retain its mana cost. If devotion was eight immediately before Saw, the two copies raise it to ten, so their two entry triggers drain each opponent for twenty total if the board stays unchanged. This does not include any earlier Merchant trigger. Lower devotion means less damage; Saw can be interrupted, and indestructible would prevent the copies.

+
Sheoldred, the Apocalypse + Dark Deal

Adds life-loss pressure to the theft turn and gains life from your own redraw. Each opponent loses two life per card they actually draw, not per card discarded. An opponent discarding seven draws six and loses twelve life if all the relevant triggers resolve; this is powerful but not an automatic table kill.

+
Sangromancer + Dark Deal

Gains three life per opposing card discarded, replenishing life for Citadel, Ring, and protection spells. Sangromancer also triggers on opposing creature deaths, including token deaths; that is broader than Tergrid's sacrifice trigger. In this deck, life is fuel rather than merely a buffer.

Torment of Hailfire

Converts Coffers mana into a multiplayer finish. Opponents choose life loss, discards, or nonland sacrifices each repetition. Tergrid collects qualifying permanents only after the spell finishes. Against an opponent at 40 with no disposable cards or nonlands, X of fourteen suffices; an opponent with resources can survive that amount. Calculate the actual board rather than assuming a large X is lethal.

+
Tinybones, Trinket Thief + Bandit's Talent

Empty hands become a finishing resource. Tinybones spends six mana to make every empty-handed opponent lose ten life; Talent's second level chips away at players with at most one card. Its third level draws extra cards for each such opponent, giving the deck something useful after discard effects run out of targets.

+
Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance + Rankle, Master of Pranks

Pocket Nuisance deals one to each opponent for each player's discard event, including yours. Its own entry can therefore deal three to each opponent if all three discard. Rankle's discard mode can create four events, while his haste, flying, sacrifice mode, and optional redraw keep pressure on a depleted table. Multiple cards discarded by one player simultaneously are only one event for Pocket Nuisance.

Tergrid, God of Fright // Tergrid's Lantern

The back face is an alternative late-game mana sink when stealing cards is no longer useful. You cannot have both faces of this one card on the battlefield, and it does not transform on demand. Cast the desired face when you can cast the card; commander tax counts previous command-zone casts of either face. The Lantern's target can always choose to lose three life instead of giving up a card or permanent.

Tutor for the current problem
→
Diabolic Intent → Withering Torment

Find the answer first when an enchantment is shutting off the theft engine. Sacrifice a spent Burglar Rat or another expendable creature; sacrificing Tergrid makes the follow-up theft plan much worse. If you need a land instead, the same tutor can find Cabal Coffers or Urborg.

→
Diabolic Intent → Bolas's Citadel

Find the independent engine once your mana, life, and immediate defense are secure. Do not spend your last expendable blocker assembling a six-mana card you cannot safely cast.

→
Beseech the Mirror → Aetherflux Reservoir

When Citadel and Top are ready, bargain a spare artifact, enchantment, or token to cast Reservoir immediately. Bargain cannot sacrifice an ordinary nontoken creature unless it is also an artifact or enchantment. A spent Virus Beetle or Hopeless Nightmare is useful material.

→
Beseech the Mirror → Sensei's Divining Top

Find and cast the missing cheap combo piece with bargain. Alternatively, use the tutor for a defensive card or draw engine rather than forcing the combo. Citadel costs six, so Beseech puts it into your hand instead of casting it for free.

Opening hands and sequencing

Keep three functional mana sources, early acceleration, and a draw engine or useful interaction. A two-land hand needs convincing acceleration and an early refill; a Coffers-heavy hand with no Swamps is not a functional keep. Mulligan expensive finishers with no development. There are 36 lands and two spell/land cards, but the land faces and spells cannot both be used from the same card.

+
Cavern of Souls + Command Beacon

Cavern naming God protects the casting of Tergrid and Aclazotz; its colored mana cannot pay for unrelated spells or the Lantern face. Beacon puts your commander into hand to avoid command-zone tax on the next cast, at the cost of a land. Neither is a normal black source for the rest of the deck without Urborg.

++++
Swamp + Arcane Signet + Waste Not + Sheoldred's Edict + Night's Whisper

An illustrative seven-card keep is three Swamps and these four spells. Turn two Signet, then use turn three for Waste Not and Night's Whisper if safe, or hold the edict against an urgent threat. With a fourth land, turn four can cast Tergrid; with only five available mana, however, that leaves no protection. Waiting to pair her with interaction is often stronger than casting her on curve.

Hopeless Nightmare

Hold the one-mana discard spell to follow Tergrid when opponents still have useful cards, unless early disruption is needed to prevent a stronger play. It also becomes bargain material later and scries two when sacrificed. Cheap effects make it easier to steal something during the same turn you expose your commander.

Before the decisive turn, count black sources, check which opponents can interact, and reserve protection. Play discard payoffs before the discard spell, preserve life for Citadel, and change to draw or drain once hands are empty. Graveyard exile can remove cards before Tergrid's triggers resolve; even returning the same card to the graveyard afterward does not restore that trigger's claim to it. This timing is confirmed in Wizards' Kaldheim release notes: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/kaldheim-release-notes-2021-01-22
