Choose the direction that leaves the least dangerous neighbor able to attack you. Pramikon protects your planeswalkers from the other opponents, but it neither stops spells nor protects you in the final duel. Develop a draw engine and a win condition before spending more cards on defenses.

Control the attack route
Pramikon, Sky Rampart

choose left or right as it enters, not through a triggered ability. Everyone can attack only their nearest opponent in that direction and that opponent's planeswalkers. Your own attackers obey the restriction too.

+
Ephemerate + Pramikon, Sky Rampart

change direction before attackers are declared, or save Pramikon from targeted removal. Changing direction after attackers are declared does not remove them from combat. An immediate blink does not save it from a sweeper still on the stack.

Propaganda

taxes attacks at you and helps keep the monarchy. It does NOT tax attacks at your planeswalkers; do not mistake this for a complete defense.

Sphere of Safety

protects both you and your walkers, scaling with the enchantments already supplying mana, cards and threats. Deploy it once there is enough enchantment density to matter.

Archangel of Tithes

while untapped, taxes attacks at you and your walkers and blocks flyers. Attacking switches off that defensive tax, so keep it back unless the offensive blocking tax helps end the game.

Norn's Annex

can come down for three mana and four life, but opponents may also pay life for their attack costs. It pressures attacks rather than preventing them.

Comeuppance

protects you and your walkers for a turn and returns prevented creature damage to the creature, or noncreature damage to the source's controller. Damage prevention fails against effects that say damage cannot be prevented.

Make the protected turns count
The Theorist, Jace Beleren

draws at the beginning of each opponent's draw step, giving three extra cards per round in a normal four-player game. Make an Illusion blocker or bounce an artifact or creature from each opponent as needed.

Teferi, Hero of Dominaria

draws while untapping two lands at the next end step, leaving mana for interaction. Its minus answers a nonland permanent; use the eventual emblem to remove threats and defenses rather than pursuing land denial.

Chandra, Torch of Defiance

adds mana to develop the board, removes a creature or offers the top card. You cannot play a land with its exile ability; declining or being unable to cast the card instead deals two to each opponent.

Chandra, Awakened Inferno

repeat the +2 to give each opponent accumulating damage emblems that remain after Chandra leaves. This is inevitability, not an instant kill. Its sweep also hits most of your own creatures.

The Wandering Emperor

flash in to exile a tapped attacker or make a blocker. Instant-speed loyalty activation is permitted only during the turn it entered; normal restrictions resume afterward.

The Eternal Wanderer

choose each player's surviving creature for the -4, usually keeping Pramikon while leaving weak opposing bodies. The one-attacker restriction protects this walker only. Its +1 returns the exiled card at its owner's next end step.

+
Elspeth, Storm Slayer + Elspeth, Sun's Champion

the first doubles tokens, so the second's +1 produces six Soldiers. Storm Slayer's 0 pumps your board and gives flying until your next turn; the other Elspeth can sweep large creatures while preserving the small army.

Lae'zel, Vlaakith's Champion

increases counters placed on your creatures and walkers, including walkers' starting loyalty and positive loyalty costs. It does not reduce a negative loyalty cost or make an activation free.

Oath of Teferi

permits two loyalty activations per walker each turn, not unlimited activations or instant-speed use. Its entrance temporarily blinks another permanent; resetting a walker loses its accumulated loyalty.

+
Deepglow Skate + Chandra, Torch of Defiance

doubling four loyalty to eight makes Chandra's -7 available if it has not used its activation. The emblem then turns subsequent spells into five damage each to any target.

+
Deepglow Skate + Teferi, Hero of Dominaria

doubling four loyalty to eight enables the emblem when an activation remains. Activating exactly -8 without extra loyalty loses Teferi, but the emblem stays.

Semester's End

protects selected nontoken creatures and walkers from a pending sweeper, returning them with an extra appropriate counter at the next end step. Reset walkers return with starting loyalty plus the extra counter, not their former loyalty. Exiled tokens will not return.

Finish the game
+
Approach of the Second Sun + Dig Through Time

after the first Approach resolves, it is seventh from the top. Dig sees seven and can put it into your hand for a later second cast. Delve helps pay for Dig, but you still need seven mana for the winning Approach and protection.

Approach of the Second Sun

the winning cast must be from your hand, must resolve and must follow an earlier cast of another spell with that name. The first cast can have been countered, but then this deck must recover the card before trying again.

Grand Abolisher

shields your winning turn from opposing spells and activated abilities of artifacts, creatures and enchantments. Opposing land abilities and triggered abilities are still possible.

Court of Ire

seven damage each upkeep while you retain the monarchy clears threats or finishes damaged players. Without the crown it deals only two; the monarch card draw is a major part of why this belongs here.

+
Court of Vantress + Court of Ire

as monarch, copy Ire to multiply future upkeep damage. The new Court entering makes you monarch, but does not retroactively trigger its upkeep ability that turn. Without the crown, Vantress can become a copy instead of creating one.

+
Court of Grace + Elspeth, Storm Slayer

produces two 4/4 Angels each upkeep while monarch, or two Spirits otherwise. These protect your walkers and become a flying win condition.

Shark Typhoon

casting noncreature spells builds flying threats while you play control. Cycling instead supplies a card and one immediate blocker; it does not leave the enchantment on the battlefield.

Cyclonic Rift

overload before your finishing turn to clear opposing nonland defenses while preserving your board. You still obey Pramikon's attack direction and may need successive combats or a noncombat finish.

The deck is built to win after establishing control, usually later than a dedicated combat deck. Approach, accumulating emblems and Court damage let it finish when attacking one opponent at a time is too slow. There are no intended infinite loops, extra-turn chains or paired direction locks.

Keep mana and cards flowing
++
Sol Ring + Arcane Signet + Talisman of Progress

early ramp makes a protected four- or five-mana walker realistic. The other signets and talismans fill the same job; prioritize the colors needed for Pramikon and double-colored spells.

The Mind Stone

a white mana rock early. Once harnessed for six mana, it can blink a nonland permanent each end step to reset a depleted walker, reclaim the monarchy with a Court or change Pramikon's direction.

+
Smothering Tithe + Elspeth, Storm Slayer

each unpaid draw makes two Treasures with Elspeth present. Banking mana lets you cast a major spell without dropping all interaction.

Gleaming Splendor

turns opponents' second draws each turn into Treasure. Its draw activation gives a card to each of two target players, so choose an opponent deliberately rather than feeding the leader automatically.

++
Rhystic Study + Mystic Remora + Esper Sentinel

early draw engines that work before the planeswalkers arrive. Do not pay Remora's rising upkeep indefinitely when doing so prevents you from developing.

Trouble in Pairs

draws from opponents' second spells, second draws and attacks at you with multiple creatures. Pramikon reduces the attack opportunities, but the other triggers still work; the extra-turn restriction also helps keep the game on your timetable.

Fact or Fiction

instant-speed selection while holding up interaction. Unlike Dig, it leaves unused cards in the graveyard, so consider recursion targets and what you can afford to lose.

Answer what the wall cannot stop
+
Swords to Plowshares + Path to Exile

exile a key creature or the attacker that would remove a walker. The life or land conceded is preferable to losing the engine.

++
Generous Gift + Chaos Warp + Sink into Stupor // Soporific Springs

broad answers to permanents your combat defenses cannot touch. Sink also returns a spell from the stack, but usually lets its owner cast it again later.

+++
Swan Song + Dovin's Veto + Mana Drain + Counterspell

reserve counters for opposing wins, engine-breaking spells or protection of your own finish. Mana Drain's delayed mana is colorless, so keep colored sources available.

+
An Offer You Can't Refuse + Deflecting Swat

cheap insurance when you develop a walker. Offer gives the opponent two Treasures; Swat changes targets and does not stop an untargeted sweeper.

+
Supreme Verdict + Sunfall

reset creature boards while leaving walkers intact. Verdict destroys and cannot be countered; Sunfall exiles and creates an Incubator. Both also remove your commander and creatures, so plan the rebuild.

Promise of Loyalty

retain one creature per player and prohibit the survivors with vow counters from attacking you or your walkers. New creatures remain free to attack, and you sacrifice your other creatures too.

+
Vandalblast + Witch Enchanter // Witch-Blessed Meadow

answer artifact engines or troublesome enchantments through Witch. Overloaded Vandalblast preserves your own artifacts; Witch can be played as a land when development matters more.

Soul-Guide Lantern

holds graveyard strategies in check without consuming a large part of your turn. Keep its activation available when a reanimation player is poised to act.

Clever Concealment

phase out the chosen nonland board through a sweeper. Tokens survive phasing; small tokens also help convoke the spell, including those that entered this turn.

Find the engine you are missing
→
Call the Gatewatch → The Theorist, Jace Beleren

find the strongest sustained refill when protection is already in place.

→
Call the Gatewatch → The Eternal Wanderer

find a creature reset that can preserve Pramikon and leave a walker behind.

→
Call the Gatewatch → Chandra, Awakened Inferno

find a noncombat clock when the board is stable but attacking cannot finish the table.

→
Idyllic Tutor → Sphere of Safety

find protection for you and the walkers when one neighbor's board is the immediate threat.

→
Idyllic Tutor → Oath of Teferi

find acceleration when two or more walkers can profit from extra activations now.

→
Idyllic Tutor → Court of Vantress

find a copying engine when a valuable enchantment or artifact is already available and the crown is defensible.

Sevinne's Reclamation

recover cheap mana, draw engines or Pramikon if you deliberately left it in the graveyard. It cannot return your four- to six-mana walkers.

Hall of Heliod's Generosity

recovers a lost enchantment to the top, where your draw engines can retrieve it. This spends mana and a draw, so prioritize a card that changes the game.

Opening hands and sequencing

Keep three usable mana sources, a cheap rock or draw engine and interaction. Avoid hands made entirely of walkers and five-mana defenses. There are 36 lands plus two spell/land cards, but Interplanar Beacon cannot pay for Pramikon with its colored-mana ability.

Interplanar Beacon

fixes walker colors and gains life when you cast one. Its colored mana is restricted to planeswalker spells; use the ordinary colorless ability for other costs.

++
Flooded Strand + Hallowed Fountain + Tundra

plan blue and white first while securing red for the commander. The mana base includes nine fetchlands, original duals, shocks and multiplayer duals; four basics remain for straightforward fetch targets and opposing basic-land search effects.

In the first turns, establish mana and cards, then choose Pramikon's direction from the actual board. Deploy a walker only when it can survive or immediately solve a problem. In the final duel, rely on removal, blockers and your finishing plan: left and right lead to the same opponent.
