Urza turns every artifact you control into a blue mana. Not artifacts that tap for mana — ALL of them: the Construct token he brings with him, Clues, Baubles, Equipment, even Sol Ring after you have already tapped it for its own mana would not work, but everything untapped does. That one line is why this deck's mana is effectively its board, and why an untap effect is the same thing as a ritual.

The commander
Urza, Lord High Artificer

arrives with a Construct that is as big as your artifact count, taps any untapped artifact for {U}, and his {5} ability exiles the top card of your library and lets you play it free.

Tapping an artifact for Urza's mana uses it up. You cannot tap Sol Ring for {C}{C} and then tap it again for Urza — pick one. That is the main thing people get wrong.

The {5} ability is the deck's engine once mana is unlimited: shuffle, exile, cast free, repeat until you find the kill.

Lightning Greaves

haste so the {5} ability works immediately, and shroud against the removal that follows him.

The infinite mana
Isochron Scepter

imprint Dramatic Reversal. {2} and a tap copies it, untapping every nonland permanent you control. If those permanents tap for three or more with Urza out — and they always do — the loop pays for itself forever.

Dramatic Reversal

the other half, and a fine card on its own for a big Urza turn.

Basalt Monolith

taps for three, untaps for three. On its own that is nothing; with either card below it is unlimited colourless mana.

Power Artifact

enchanted artifact's activated abilities cost {2} less. Basalt Monolith now untaps for {1} and taps for three.

Forensic Gadgeteer

the same reduction across ALL your artifacts, and it makes a Clue on every artifact spell.

Rings of Brighthearth

copies any nonmana activated ability for {2}, including Urza's {5}, Tezzeret's minus and Walking Ballista's ping.

What unlimited mana buys
Walking Ballista

{4} for a counter, remove a counter to ping. With infinite mana it kills the table, and with none it is still a flexible removal creature.

Urza, Lord High Artificer

the {5} ability, over and over, casting your entire library for free.

Whir of Invention

Reshape

Transmute Artifact

trades a big artifact for whatever you need.

Tezzeret the Seeker

the minus finds any artifact onto the battlefield; the plus untaps two.

Winning without the combo

This is the answer to "does it just run out of gas" — it does not, and it has a fair kill.

Cyberdrive Awakener

every noncreature artifact becomes a 4/4 AND they all fly. On a board of twelve artifacts that is a lethal alpha strike from nowhere.

Tezzeret the Seeker

the ultimate does the same for a turn.

The Construct token Urza brings with him is as big as your artifact count — by turn six that is eight or ten power on a single body, and Cyberdrive Awakener gives it flying.

Mechanized Production

enchant an artifact you have several copies of — or one that copies itself — and it makes another every upkeep. At eight with the same name, you simply win.

Sai, Master Thopterist

a flying Thopter for every artifact spell, and a sacrifice outlet that draws.

Card flow
The One Ring

a full turn of protection, then it draws an escalating pile — the single best card in the deck for a grindy game.

Mystic Forge

Sensei's Divining Top

sets your draws, and it is an artifact that taps for {U} under Urza.

The Reality Chip

Padeem, Consul of Innovation

Mystic Remora

Faerie Mastermind

Timetwister

What to keep in an opening hand

Keep cheap artifacts and a land. Mox Opal, Lotus Petal, Mishra's Bauble and Sol Ring all make Urza a turn-three play and immediately turn into mana once he lands.

Count Urza's mana correctly. Each untapped artifact is one blue, once. Tapping Sol Ring for its own two means it is no longer available for Urza — work out which use you need before you start.

Muddle the Mixture

remember it transmutes for exactly two. Isochron Scepter, Basalt Monolith and Power Artifact are all two, so it is a tutor for whichever combo half you are missing.

Hold up interaction on the turn you go off. The deck's failure case is a single counterspell on Dramatic Reversal, and Force of Negation or Pact of Negation costs you nothing to keep available.
