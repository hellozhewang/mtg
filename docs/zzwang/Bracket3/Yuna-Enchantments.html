This is an enchantment-combo deck with a real graveyard engine. Cheap enchantresses and mana enchantments develop the hand; Yuna returns the best enchantment at your end step while compact Heliod, Earthcraft and Sanctum Weaver lines finish without requiring an eight-mana Summon to survive several turns. Keep the strongest Summons as recursion targets rather than filling the curve with expensive payoffs. The Game Changers are Enlightened Tutor, Survival of the Fittest and Serra's Sanctum. Hold compact wins and infinite loops until turn six under the house rules. No numerical goldfish win rate is claimed.

Opening hands and turn planning

Keep two or three usable sources, early green mana, and an accelerator plus an enchantress or tutor. Wild Growth or a one-mana creature followed by Sythis, an enchantress or Survival is better than a hand of large Summons with no way to discard them. Serra's Sanctum produces nothing with no enchantments; a lone Sanctum is not an opening mana source. Bala Ged Recovery and Witch Enchanter can be lands, but then they are not available as spells.

Turns one and two establish mana and cards. Turns three and four find the missing engine or set up the graveyard while holding an answer. Yuna is useful when her first end step returns something meaningful; do not automatically spend five mana on her instead of completing a cheaper combo. Starting on turn six, prioritize a protected complete finish over another value permanent.

For graveyard setups, discard the enchantment BEFORE the end step begins. Yuna's target is selected when her trigger is put on the stack; discarding a different card in response does not let you change that target. Graveyard hate can remove the target in response, so sometimes the correct plan is to cast the combo from hand instead.

Compact finish one — Heliod and Ballista
+
Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Walking Ballista

With Heliod already out, cast Ballista for X=2, costing four mana, and pay {1}{W} to grant lifelink. Remove one counter for one damage, gain one life, and resolve Heliod's trigger putting a counter back on Ballista. Repeat, one shot and reload at a time, until all opponents lose. Heliod does not need to be a creature. Keep at least one counter on an unboosted Ballista while the shot is waiting to resolve; X=1 is not the normal starting state because spending its last counter kills it before it can be reloaded.

Walking Ballista

A fresh Ballista can shoot because its damage ability has no tap cost. The card has mana value zero in the library, but its normal cast cost is twice X. A creature tutor that puts it straight onto the battlefield for X=0 does not give it counters. Yuna's own lifelink effect does NOT cover Ballista: Ballista is an artifact creature, not an enchantment creature.

+
Power Conduit + Walking Ballista

Conduit can provide a second counter to a surviving X=1 Ballista when you have a counter to remove elsewhere. That is a specific setup, not permission to start the Heliod loop with only one counter. Removing a finality or lore counter as the cost does not target the source permanent; you can put a charge counter on Conduit itself if your enchantment creatures have shroud.

Compact finish two — Earthcraft, Nest and Station
++
Earthcraft + Squirrel Nest + Blasting Station

Enchant a BASIC land with Nest. Tap the land to create a Squirrel, resolve Station's untap trigger, then tap that new Squirrel to Earthcraft to untap the enchanted basic. Tap Station and sacrifice the now-tapped Squirrel to deal one damage. The land is untapped again; making the next Squirrel untaps Station again. Repeat 40 times per opponent at 40 life, or a larger finite number as needed. No haste, preexisting army, commander or extra untap spell is required. The new Squirrel can pay Earthcraft because it is not activating its own tap ability.

+
Earthcraft + Squirrel Nest

Without a damage outlet the loop makes arbitrarily many Squirrels, but most end up tapped and they do not automatically attack this turn. Find Station instead of declaring the army an immediate pod kill. A dual land with the Forest type is not a basic land, and Yavimaya does not add the basic supertype. Use an actual Forest or Plains for the loop.

Blasting Station

The sacrifice ability deals one damage regardless of the sacrificed creature's power. Its untap trigger is optional, and a creature entering is enough to trigger it. It can sacrifice an expendable Summon in response to removal or to put a finality-free enchantment into the graveyard, but a finality counter sends that sacrifice to exile instead. Station does not bypass indestructible on opposing targets; the combo wins by dealing lethal damage to players.

Compact finish three — enchantment mana into Ballista
+
Sanctum Weaver + Gauntlets of Light

With at least FOUR enchantments, including Weaver and Gauntlets, and Weaver able to tap, tap for four white, pay {2}{W} to untap Weaver and bank one white. Repeat. Bank white untap costs before tapping for other colors to generate arbitrary amounts of each useful color. At only three enchantments the loop breaks even and is not infinite mana. Yuna herself is not an enchantment and does not count toward this threshold.

+
Sanctum Weaver + Staff of Domination

With at least FIVE enchantments and a ready Weaver, tap for at least five mana. Pay three and tap Staff to untap Weaver, then pay one to untap Staff: net at least one mana per cycle. Four enchantments only breaks even. Staff is not an enchantment, so it does not increase Weaver's output.

+
Sanctum Weaver + Walking Ballista

Once a mana-positive untap engine is actually assembled, cast Ballista for X at least 120 against three opponents at 40. That costs at least 240 mana. If Ballista is already on the battlefield, add counters for four mana each. If Ballista is missing, use a tutor or Staff that is genuinely accessible; mana alone does not search the library.

Staff of Domination

With unlimited mana, drawing costs five and a tap, then one to untap Staff. Draw only until the outlet or needed tutor is found, not into an empty library. Staff can also tap blockers or gain life as a fallback, but neither is automatically a win.

→
Finale of Devastation → Walking Ballista

With unlimited mana, Finale for X at least ten puts ONE Ballista from library or graveyard onto the battlefield with a temporary toughness bonus. Use its four-mana ability to add at least 120 counters while that bonus keeps it alive, then shoot. A low-X Finale does not preserve an ordinary zero-counter Ballista. Finale grants haste and a power bonus to your team, but does not find all the creatures in your library.

Yuna recursion — exact finality and Saga timing
Yuna, Hope of Spira

On your turn, only Yuna and your enchantment creatures gain trample, lifelink and ward {2}. Ordinary Squirrel, Knight and Spirit tokens do not receive those abilities just because this deck created them. The protection ends on other players' turns. At the beginning of your end step, return up to one target enchantment card from the graveyard with a finality counter; this includes noncreature enchantments such as Survival or Earthcraft.

Power Conduit

Remove a returned enchantment's FINALITY counter before letting it die if you want to recur it again. Conduit can do this immediately after the return even if Conduit entered this turn, because it is normally not a creature. The ability taps, so one Conduit is one removal per untap. A removed finality counter does not exile the permanent; leaving it on does not prevent sacrifice, but replaces the trip to the graveyard with exile.

Nesting Grounds

Pay {1} and tap at sorcery speed to MOVE a finality counter onto another permanent, preferably an expendable token or suitable opposing permanent. It does not simply delete the counter. A newly returned end-step Summon normally waits until your next main phase to use Grounds; removal before then can still exile it. Grounds cannot act while a chapter ability is on the stack.

+
Summon: Bahamut + Power Conduit

Bahamut enters with chapter I, then gains lore as each of your first main phases begins. To repeat chapter IV, let that chapter trigger, then remove a LORE counter with Conduit while the trigger is on the stack. Mega Flare still resolves, but the Saga is below its final chapter number afterward and survives. Repeat on later turns if Conduit untaps. Mega Flare counts the total mana value of your OTHER permanents; Bahamut's own nine is excluded. This is a repeatable later-turn damage engine, not an immediate kill when Bahamut first returns.

+
Summon: Knights of Round + Power Conduit

Each of chapters I through IV makes three Knights. Once chapter V is on the stack, removing a lore counter lets that chapter resolve while preserving the Saga to repeat V on later turns. If you want fresh Knights instead, reduce lore earlier to repeat a token chapter. Removing lore by itself does not trigger the chapter you moved back to. Keeping lore low and removing finality are different uses of Conduit's one tap.

Summon: Yojimbo

Chapter I permanently exiles an opposing artifact, enchantment or tapped creature; it does not return that card when Yojimbo leaves. Chapters II and III tax attacks until your next turn. Recur it for another first-chapter answer once it is back in the graveyard without finality. It cannot answer an untapped creature through that chapter.

Summon: Fenrir

A cheap enchantment creature with a basic-land search on chapter I. Its later counter setup and conditional draw are incidental value. Yuna can return it to restart the land search after it dies without finality, but that recurrence still happens only once each of your end steps.

Touch the Spirit Realm

Cast it as an enchantment to temporarily exile an artifact or creature, or channel for {1}{W} to blink one until the next end step. Blinking a Summon removes finality and resets its lore as a new object; the return does not add another finality counter by itself. Channel before the end step begins if you want the return in that next end step. Blinking Yuna makes her a new object and may miss her end-step trigger; use that protection with timing in mind.

The old universal counter-prevention plan is gone: preventing lore counters also prevents new Summons from reaching their chapters. Finality management here uses removal, movement, blinking or accepting a one-use reanimation. Finality also stops normal dies triggers when it replaces death with exile; do not assume every returned creature automatically reaches the graveyard.

Put the right enchantment in the right zone
→
Survival of the Fittest → Sanctum Weaver

Pay {G} and discard a CREATURE to find any creature to hand. Discard a large Summon to prepare Yuna's end step while finding Weaver, Heliod, Ballista, an enchantress or a protective creature. Each activation costs green and a new creature card; it is not a free chain or a way to discard an arbitrary noncreature enchantment.

→
Pia, Aether Ascetic → Earthcraft

Discard ANY card on entry to find an enchantment to hand: Earthcraft, Nest, Gauntlets, Heliod, a draw engine or a Summon. Discarding Bahamut can both fuel Yuna and find an engine. Pia cannot directly find Ballista, Station or Staff.

→
Formidable Speaker → Walking Ballista

Entry plus a discarded card finds any creature to hand. Fauna Shaman performs the creature-discard search repeatedly, but costs {G}, a tap and a creature card each time; give it a turn to become ready. Speaker's separate untap ability also requires its tap and one mana.

→
Enlightened Tutor → Blasting Station

Top-deck access to every artifact or enchantment combo piece, including Ballista, Staff, Weaver, Heliod, Earthcraft and Nest. Account for a natural or paid draw. Complete other shuffling searches first so a fetch does not erase the top-deck setup.

→
Sterling Grove → Squirrel Nest

Pay one and sacrifice Grove for an enchantment on TOP. It can find Earthcraft, Gauntlets, Heliod or a draw engine; it cannot find Replenish, which is a sorcery. Yuna can return a sacrificed Grove later, but a returned Grove has finality and needs counter removal before another sacrifice if you want it back again.

+
Sterling Grove + Sanctum Weaver

Grove gives OTHER enchantments shroud, including Weaver. Sacrifice Grove before casting Gauntlets targeting Weaver, before targeting your enchantments with Calix counters, or before a Staff activation that targets Weaver. Gauntlets' granted self-untap does not target, so Grove can protect a Weaver that is already enchanted. An Aura returned by Yuna or Replenish attaches without targeting, so it may attach to an existing shrouded Weaver if otherwise legal. Grove does not protect the nonenchantment Yuna.

→
Idyllic Tutor → Gauntlets of Light

Three-mana straight-to-hand enchantment tutor. With a ready Weaver and enough enchantments, Gauntlets is the missing untapper; with no draw engine, an enchantress may be the better early target. Do not get an expensive Summon instead of a complete protected kill.

→
Eladamri's Call → Heliod, Sun-Crowned

Finds any creature to hand for two mana, including Heliod, Ballista, Weaver or Grand Abolisher. Ranger-Captain finds Ballista through mana value zero, then offers a sacrifice that stops opposing noncreature spells for the turn.

→
Green Sun's Zenith → Sanctum Weaver

X=2 finds Weaver, Sythis or Destiny Spinner directly into play. It cannot find the white Heliod or colorless Ballista. Nature's Rhythm can find any creature, but a zero-counter Ballista normally dies immediately. Its harmonize option can reuse the tutor from the graveyard, with four green pips still payable.

Malevolent Rumble

Look at four, take a permanent, put the rest in the graveyard and make a Spawn that sacrifices for {C}. It can find a land, artifact or enchantment while preparing a Yuna target. If Ballista goes to the graveyard, Yuna cannot return it; use Finale's large-X line or Bala Ged Recovery instead.

Commune with the Gods

Look at five for a creature or enchantment to hand, with the remainder in the graveyard. It cannot take a land or an ordinary artifact. Use it to find an engine and seed a valuable Summon rather than treating it as an unrestricted tutor.

Replenish

Returns all enchantment CARDS from the graveyard, without adding finality counters. Enchantresses that trigger on CAST do not trigger; Eidolon and Setessan Champion do trigger on enchantments entering. Auras must attach legally to something already available as they enter: a Gauntlets returning in the same batch cannot attach to a Weaver also returning in that batch. If nothing legal exists, that Aura stays in the graveyard. Grove returning simultaneously can create further targeting restrictions after entry; resolve the rebuild carefully.

+
Bala Ged Recovery // Bala Ged Sanctuary + Hall of Heliod's Generosity

Recovery returns any card to hand. Hall pays {1}{W} and taps for an enchantment on top. Neither retrieves a card already exiled by finality. Keep the best recursion for a missing combo piece rather than spending it on the first value card that dies.

Engines, protection and alternate pressure
+
Sythis, Harvest's Hand + Enchantress's Presence

Sythis gains life and draws on enchantment CASTS; Presence and Argothian Enchantress also draw on casts. These help through counters but do not draw from Yuna simply returning a card. Avoid casting extra enchantments into an empty library when a mandatory draw engine is active.

+
Eidolon of Blossoms + Setessan Champion

Constellation draws from enchantments ENTERING, including Yuna returns and noncreature enchantments. Champion also grows. This is why reanimation and Replenish are genuine recovery engines even when casting is constrained.

Hallowed Haunting

Every enchantment spell you cast makes a Spirit, not just enchantment-creature spells. With seven enchantments the whole team gains flying and vigilance, giving a credible combat plan when the combo is interrupted. The Spirit tokens scale with your Spirit count but are not enchantment creatures themselves.

Calix, Guided by Fate

Constellation puts a counter on a creature. Once each turn, Calix or an enchanted creature connecting can copy a NONLEGENDARY enchantment: Weaver, a draw engine or a Summon are useful choices. Calix cannot copy legendary Heliod. Another Survival is legal but usually less useful than another mana or draw engine. The combat trigger is not one copy per attacker. A copied Saga starts with its own lore, not the original's counters.

Greater Good

Sacrifice a large creature for cards, then discard three, ideally seeding Yuna targets. Bahamut at power nine draws nine and discards three. Sacrificing zero-power Weaver instead draws zero and still discards three, so do not call every creature sacrifice card advantage. Finality can exile the sacrificed creature while the draw ability still resolves; it does not turn that into a reusable graveyard target.

+
Serra's Sanctum + Sanctum Weaver

These are explosive mana once enchantments are established; count the real permanents, not cards in the graveyard or ordinary creature tokens. Fetch basics for Earthcraft and keep both colors available. Wild Growth and Utopia Sprawl accelerate early while raising enchantment count; Sprawl needs a Forest, and choosing white can fix the second color.

Enduring Vitality

Turns your creatures into mana sources, but fresh creatures are still summoning sick for that granted tap ability. Its normal death can return it as a noncreature enchantment. A finality counter instead exiles it, so there is no dies trigger to bring it back. It is support for the board, not a substitute for a mana-positive untap loop.

Destiny Spinner

Makes your creature and enchantment SPELLS uncounterable, not every spell or activated ability. With mana, it can animate lands with trample and haste for combat. Each activation sets power/toughness to the enchantment count; repeated activations do not add arbitrary power to one land. Count actual combat damage across three opponents before declaring a win.

+
Grand Abolisher + Silence

Abolisher also blocks opposing artifact, creature and enchantment activations on your turn; Silence blocks only spells after it resolves. Ranger-Captain blocks only noncreature spells. None removes abilities already on the stack or all triggered abilities, and opponents can answer the protection before it resolves.

+
Veil of Summer + Heroic Intervention

Veil addresses countering and blue/black targeting; Intervention supplies hexproof and indestructible. Neither universally stops sacrifice or exile sweepers. Force of Vigor is free only on opponents' turns; on your own turn it costs {2}{G}{G}. Keep a real answer to the particular hate piece in front of you.

++
Nature's Claim + Aura of Silence + Seal of Cleansing

Remove artifact/enchantment hate before the combo. Aura and Seal can be returned by Yuna, but a finality counter makes a later sacrifice exile them unless removed first. Creature-activation hate stops Weaver and Ballista, while the Earthcraft/Nest engine still works because Earthcraft supplies its ability. Artifact-activation hate stops Station, Ballista, Staff and Conduit. Use the appropriate removal, or take the genuine combat route instead.

Against graveyard hate, use hand/library tutors and the independent combo rather than forcing a five-mana Yuna reanimation. Against a creature sweeper, preserve an enchantress or recovery spell instead of exposing every mana creature. Against repeated commander removal, make the opponents answer Earthcraft or Heliod: the deck is built to finish without recasting Yuna indefinitely.
