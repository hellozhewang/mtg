Do not spend all seven mana on Lim-Dûl and expect him to take over. He needs a creature to die and another two mana per creature you want to steal. Develop mana, draw, and removal first; the commander turns an operating control engine into a source of stolen threats.

The requested 3.9 is interpreted as the upper end of Bracket 3, not a separate official bracket. This list uses three Game Changers, no planned infinite loop, no mass land denial, and no extra-turn chain. Its power comes from scalable mana, repeated sacrifice effects, and explosive reanimation. A seven-mana commander remains a real constraint: this is not a promise to race optimized spell-combo decks.

Understand what the commander can actually steal

Lim-Dûl the Necromancer

Pays {1}{B} separately as each death trigger resolves to return that creature card under your control. The creature must have been controlled by an opponent immediately before it died. A stolen creature that later dies under your control does not trigger him again, even though an opponent owns it. Tokens cannot return, and a commander moved to the command zone is no longer available in the graveyard.

He sees opposing creatures die alongside him in a board wipe, so those triggers can return creatures even after he has died. Exile and bounce do not count as dying, and removing the card from the graveyard before his trigger resolves stops the return. Decide which triggers deserve mana rather than buying every small creature.

His regeneration ability targets Zombies. Lim-Dûl is naturally a Human Wizard and cannot regenerate himself. Regeneration prevents destruction or lethal damage only when a shield is established beforehand; it does not stop sacrifice, exile, or toughness falling to zero. Regenerating a creature also taps it and removes it from combat.

Build enough mana to cast threats and still steal creatures

Cabal Coffers

The primary scalable mana source. Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth makes every land you control a Swamp, including Coffers itself, so the engine scales beyond the basic-land count. Early Coffers without enough Swamps can be worse than a normal land.

Deserted Temple

Untaps Coffers for another activation once the land count makes the extra activation profitable. You still pay both the Temple's activation and Coffers' activation costs; this pair is finite mana, not an infinite loop.

Thespian's Stage

Copies Coffers once the first copy is profitable. Vesuva provides another copy but enters tapped; use either as ordinary mana development when waiting for the perfect copy target would stall you.

Cabal Stronghold

A secondary engine supported by 23 basic Swamps. It counts basic Swamps only, so Urborg does not make every nonbasic land count toward its output.

Crypt Ghast

Adds black mana when a Swamp is tapped for mana, including a Coffers that Urborg has made a Swamp. It adds one extra mana per qualifying tap, not a second copy of the entire Coffers output. Extort helps recover life spent on cards and reanimation.

Nirkana Revenant

A second Swamp-based mana engine that can also become a large attacker. It is expensive, so reanimate it when the additional mana immediately enables a stronger follow-up or several theft triggers.

Bubbling Muck

Converts one developed turn into enough mana for a large Torment of Hailfire or several spells. It helps every player's Swamps for the turn; with Urborg present, opponents also receive extra black mana when tapping their lands.

Lake of the Dead

Sacrifices a Swamp just to enter and another to produce four black mana through its larger ability. Use the burst for a decisive play, rather than repeatedly reducing your future Coffers output for minor value.

The Soul Stone

Reliable two-mana acceleration early, with a late option to harness it for upkeep reanimation. Harnessing exiles your creature instead of sacrificing it, so it does not trigger death abilities. The infinity symbol labels its harnessed ability; it does not mean this card creates an infinite combo.

Pitiless Plunderer

Turns your other creatures dying into Treasures, letting sacrifice effects help pay for Lim-Dûl's theft triggers. It does not pay you when opposing creatures die unless those creatures have first become yours.

Ashnod's Altar

Converts expendable bodies into mana at instant speed and starts Grave Pact triggers without requiring a spell. Its mana is colorless, so you still need a black source for every Lim-Dûl payment.

Grim Hireling

Rewards connecting with multiple opponents by making two Treasures per damaged player. Its sorcery-speed removal ability turns accumulated Treasures into a way to kill and potentially steal a valuable creature.

Keep the sacrifice engine supplied

Ophiomancer

Makes a Snake on each upkeep if you have none. Sacrifice the old Snake before the next upkeep begins to create another opportunity; it cannot manufacture another Snake later in an upkeep after the trigger condition was missed.

Jadar, Ghoulcaller of Nephalia

Supplies a decayed Zombie at your end step when you have no decayed creatures. Use the token as sacrifice material; it cannot block. With Braids, spend an existing token before the end step if you want Jadar to trigger, then resolve Jadar's creation before Braids if you want to sacrifice the new token.

Grave Titan

Provides a substantial threat and two Zombies immediately, then more bodies on each attack. It supplies sacrifice material even against opponents who play very few creatures.

Viscera Seer

A free sacrifice outlet that turns doomed creatures into selection and starts Grave Pact triggers. With Bolas's Citadel, scry away an unwanted top card when you have a creature to spend.

Grave Pact

Each of your creatures dying makes every other player sacrifice a creature. With a token source and sacrifice outlet, this restricts opposing boards while giving Lim-Dûl multiple theft opportunities. Opponents choose what they sacrifice, and the engine requires continuing fodder.

Dictate of Erebos

Offers the same death-to-sacrifice conversion with flash. Deploy it before your creatures die to surprise the table; casting it after they have already died does not create retroactive triggers.

Draw cards before committing your expensive threats

The One Ring

A Game Changer devoted to independent draw and a turn of protection when cast. Protection does not stop its upkeep life loss. If burden counters become dangerous, sacrifice it to Braids or bargain it to Beseech the Mirror; indestructible does not prevent sacrifice.

Black Market Connections

Supplies cards, Treasures, and optional bodies. Taking every mode costs six life per turn, so choose based on actual needs and the life required for reanimation or Citadel.

Skullclamp

Turns a Snake or another unboosted 1/1 into two cards immediately. A 2/2 Zombie survives the equipment's toughness reduction; equip it and then sacrifice it to an outlet to get the cards.

Braids, Arisen Nightmare

Converts a spare permanent into opposing sacrifices or your own cards. Opponents choose, so it does not guarantee three draws. Sacrificing a creature also triggers Grave Pact independently of what they choose for Braids.

Morbid Opportunist

Draws once per turn when another creature dies, including an opponent's creature or a token. Use sacrifice outlets across different turns to get more than one card per round; a mass death event still gives only one trigger that turn.

Yawgmoth, Thran Physician

Converts creatures and life into cards while putting -1/-1 counters on opposing creatures. His counter can finish a small creature for Lim-Dûl to steal. You may choose no target. His protection from Humans prevents his own ability from targeting himself, but does not prevent him from targeting opposing Humans.

Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER // Sephiroth, One-Winged Angel

Converts another creature into a card on entry or attack and drains a target opponent when other creatures die. Four resolutions of that death ability in one turn transform him and produce a lasting drain emblem. His front face counts opposing deaths too, making sweepers particularly effective.

Reanimate the effect you need rather than the largest body

Entomb

Finds the specific creature you want to reanimate: Archon of Cruelty for immediate pressure and cards, Meteor Golem for a difficult permanent, or Gray Merchant of Asphodel for life and a finish. It does not place the card in your hand, so pair it with actual graveyard access.

Buried Alive

Sets up multiple creatures for Victimize or Living Death. Choose a mana engine, a useful entry effect, and a payoff according to the board rather than putting three expensive attackers into the graveyard by habit.

Reanimate

Retrieves a creature from any graveyard for one mana, including opponents' creatures that died before Lim-Dûl arrived. The life payment equals its mana value, so do not reanimate an eight-mana threat when that payment leaves you unable to survive.

Animate Dead

Reanimates from either side of the table without the large life payment. Removing the Aura forces the creature's sacrifice; stealing a creature this way does not automatically make it a Zombie for Lim-Dûl's regeneration ability.

Necromancy

A third way to use any graveyard, with an emergency instant-speed mode. When cast at a time a sorcery could not be cast, it is sacrificed at the next cleanup step, and the returned creature is normally sacrificed with it. Plan to exploit the entry effect or use a sacrifice outlet before then.

Victimize

Trades one expendable creature for two creatures from your graveyard, returned tapped. It targets two cards before resolution, so establish a stocked graveyard and a creature you can actually sacrifice.

Twilight Diviner

Once each turn, copies one other creature that enters under your control from a graveyard or was cast from a graveyard. Lim-Dûl's stolen creatures qualify even though they came from an opponent's graveyard. Reanimating Gray Merchant produces a copy and another drain trigger; copying a legendary creature still invokes the legend rule. Diviner cannot copy itself.

Grave Researcher // Reanimate

Surveils on your upkeep and becomes prepared when your graveyard has at least three creature cards. Once prepared, you can cast its Reanimate copy for {B}, following sorcery timing and unpreparing the Researcher. It is a repeatable engine after setup, not an immediate one-mana reanimation spell from your opening hand.

Living Death

Exiles existing graveyards, sacrifices the battlefield, then returns the initially exiled creatures for everyone. Sacrifice your own useful creatures before casting it if you want them returned by the spell. An existing Lim-Dûl sees opposing creatures sacrificed during resolution and can return those newly dead cards afterward, even if he died too. A Lim-Dûl only arriving from the graveyard afterward did not see those deaths.

Agadeem's Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt

Functions as a land when early development requires it and as mass recovery later. Returned creatures must have different mana values, each at most X; it cannot return several three-drops at once.

Remove threats and collect what remains

Orcish Bowmasters

The third Game Changer provides efficient punishment for extra draws, a sacrifice body, and damage to small creatures. A creature killed by that damage can trigger Lim-Dûl, but one damage does not reliably remove large threats.

Accursed Marauder

Each player must sacrifice a nontoken creature, so opponents cannot shelter a key threat behind ordinary tokens. The requirement applies to you too; sacrificing the Marauder itself is often the correct choice.

Soul Shatter

An instant-speed edict that pressures each opponent's most expensive creature or planeswalker. It is useful through hexproof because it does not target, although an opponent can choose a planeswalker instead of supplying a creature for Lim-Dûl.

Flare of Malice

Offers the same kind of broad edict when mana is tapped out by sacrificing a nontoken black creature. A Snake, Zombie token, or Army token cannot pay the alternative cost.

Toxic Deluge

Controls the size of a wipe and handles indestructible creatures through toughness reduction. It affects your creatures too, and Lim-Dûl's regeneration cannot save him from lethal -X/-X. Newly returned creatures are new objects and do not inherit the reduction applied when Deluge resolved.

Blasphemous Edict

Can cost just one black mana when thirteen creatures are present across the whole battlefield. It makes you sacrifice creatures too, but Lim-Dûl can still see opponents' creatures die alongside him. Reserve mana for the resulting theft triggers instead of spending it all on setup.

Withering Torment

Answers a creature or enchantment at instant speed, covering an important mono-black weakness. Feed the Swarm provides another enchantment answer at sorcery speed; prioritize effects stopping graveyard access or triggered abilities.

Meteor Golem

Gives the reanimation package an answer to an opposing artifact or another nonland permanent. It is expensive to cast, and both it and Blast Zone use destruction, so indestructible noncreature artifacts remain a weakness. Do not assume the reanimation package solves every permanent.

Soul-Guide Lantern

Selectively removes one graveyard card on entry, or clears opposing graveyards before Living Death. Activating its mass exile also removes creatures you hoped to steal or reanimate, so do not use it carelessly with Lim-Dûl triggers waiting. It leaves your own graveyard intact.

Finish with drains, entry effects, or a stolen army

Gray Merchant of Asphodel

Drains all three opponents using your black devotion and gains the total life lost. Grave Pact, Dictate of Erebos, and your other black permanents improve the drain. Reanimation and Twilight Diviner let the same card produce several large swings without an infinite loop.

Saw in Half

Destroys your Gray Merchant to create two copies with its black mana symbols, generating two more entry triggers and additional devotion. It also doubles the entry effects of Archon of Cruelty, Grave Titan, or Meteor Golem. This is a finite burst, and making two copies of an opponent's creature normally gives those copies to that opponent.

Massacre Wurm

Clears small opposing creatures and causes two life loss per opposing creature death while it is present. Token-heavy boards are especially vulnerable. Reanimation or Saw in Half can produce additional entry reductions and multiple instances of the death trigger.

Syr Konrad, the Grim

Converts creature deaths, creature milling, and creature cards leaving your graveyard into damage to every opponent. A card leaving an opponent's graveyard alone does not trigger him. His milling ability is a finite mana sink that can stock reanimation targets while pressuring life totals.

Torment of Hailfire

Turns Coffers mana into a table-wide finish after removal has reduced opponents' spare permanents. It is not simply three times X guaranteed life loss: opponents can discard or sacrifice to survive, so count their resources before committing.

Bolas's Citadel

Spends life to cast spells from the top while preserving mana for Lim-Dûl's triggers. Gray Merchant replenishes the life, and Viscera Seer or draw spells clear unwanted top cards. Its ten-permanent sacrifice drains every opponent for ten, but the deck has no planned infinite Citadel loop. Casting Torment from the top through Citadel forces X to zero, so put it into your hand for a real finishing cast.

Archon of Cruelty

Combines an evasive threat, life recovery, card draw, and an opposing sacrifice on entry and attack. It affects one target opponent per trigger, not the entire table; choose the player whose board and hand most need reducing.

Sepulchral Primordial

Reanimates up to one creature from each opponent's graveyard on entry. It is the late-game theft threat when Lim-Dûl has become too expensive or died before the important creatures did.

Sheoldred // The True Scriptures

Starts with an opposing nontoken-creature-or-planeswalker sacrifice and can become a saga once an opponent has eight cards in their graveyard. The final chapter takes all creature cards from all graveyards. It takes multiple turns to reach that chapter normally, so treat it as a must-answer threat rather than an immediate win.

Protect the engine and find the missing piece

Imp's Mischief

Redirects a spell with exactly one target when another legal target exists, helping against targeted removal or certain counterspells. It does not answer abilities or untargeted sweepers, and the life payment matters.

Malakir Rebirth // Malakir Mire

Can save one of your own creatures from dying or provide a tapped land early. The creature returns under its owner's control, so using it on a stolen opposing creature gives that creature back. Not Dead After All has the same ownership restriction. Leave your commander in the graveyard if you intend the granted death trigger to return it.

Lightning Greaves

Protects Lim-Dûl from targeting once equipped, but shroud also prevents your own targeted protection or Saw in Half. Moving equipment uses sorcery timing; you cannot move it in response to removal to rescue another creature.

Diabolic Intent

Turns a spare body into any needed card and also triggers your death engines. Find mana when you cannot finance theft, a removal spell when you are behind, or a finisher when your board already supplies resources.

Beseech the Mirror

Bargain a Treasure, token, or expendable permanent to cast a tutored spell with mana value four or less. That can find Grave Pact, The One Ring, or a cheap reanimation spell immediately. Larger cards go to your hand and still need to be cast normally.

Expedition Map

Finds Coffers, Urborg, or a utility land. If your opening mana is poor, find an immediate functional source instead of assembling a late-game engine that leaves spells stranded.

What to keep and how to sequence

Keep three functional mana sources, early acceleration, and a draw or interaction piece. Cabal Coffers without enough Swamps is not a reliable early source; multiple colorless utility lands can also strand the deck's black-heavy spells. The list has 36 lands plus Agadeem's Awakening and Malakir Rebirth as two additional land options.

An example keep is two Swamps, Castle Locthwain, Arcane Signet, Ophiomancer, Skullclamp, and Withering Torment. Develop Signet, establish the Snake-and-Clamp engine, and hold removal when an opposing threat demands it. This hand does useful work without drawing the commander or a reanimation target.

In the early turns, prioritize mana and cards. In the middle turns, establish an outlet and sacrifice payoff, or reanimate a threat with an immediate entry effect. Cast Lim-Dûl when you can realistically cause or exploit a death and still pay for the return. Seven available mana alone is not a reason to cast him.

Creature decks give this commander the most material. Against spell-combo decks, pressure life totals with your own threats, hold instant-speed interaction for key creatures, and use Soul-Guide Lantern against graveyard lines. Mono-black has limited direct stack interaction, so the deck cannot reliably control three spell-heavy opponents simply by accumulating removal.

Before a big turn, count black mana separately from colorless mana, check your remaining life, and inspect all graveyards. Clear opposing graveyard hate before committing recursion. Once you have enough resources, use Gray Merchant, Torment, Citadel's sacrifice ability, or combat to close instead of repeatedly buying low-impact creatures and extending the game.
