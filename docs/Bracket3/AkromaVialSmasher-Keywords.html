Akroma boosts OTHER creatures, not herself. Her trigger checks their abilities when it resolves, so share keywords before calculating the bonus. This is a Mardu combat deck with Vial Smasher supplying damage from development and interaction, not a deck that must cast seven-mana spells every turn. Akroma, Vision of Ixidor and Vial Smasher the Fierce are both commanders; designate both when importing. Tymna and Kediss are ordinary cards in the deck despite also having partner.

The two commanders and the trigger order

Akroma, Vision of Ixidor

Each other creature gets +1/+1 for each listed ability it has when her beginning-of-combat trigger resolves. Multiple instances of protection count once; first strike and double strike are separate listed abilities, but having both does not create a third damage step. Ward, flash, myriad, and shroud do not count. The bonus lasts until end of turn and triggers in opponents' combats too.

Vial Smasher the Fierce

Your first spell EACH turn triggers damage equal to that spell's mana value. Choose the opponent randomly as the ability resolves; you cannot aim the damage at whichever opponent you prefer. Casting Vial Smasher does not trigger herself, and casting another spell afterward does not count as a new first spell. This damage is noncombat damage and never counts toward a commander-damage kill.

Odric, Lunarch Marshal

Put Akroma's trigger on the stack first and Odric's above it, so Odric shares abilities before Akroma counts them. Akroma herself supplies flying, first strike, vigilance, and trample to the team through Odric. He can also share double strike, deathtouch, haste, hexproof, indestructible, lifelink, menace, and reach when present, but does not share partner or protection.

Akroma's Will

With either commander in play as you cast it, choose both modes. Cast it before Akroma's trigger resolves to add its six listed keyword categories to the team's available abilities, avoiding double-counting abilities creatures already have. It is both a finishing spell and protection. Protection from every colour does not prevent colourless blockers or untargeted exile effects.

Efficient creatures that become lethal together

Serra Ascendant

A one-mana lifelinker that is a 6/6 flier while you have at least thirty life. Akroma adds two more power and toughness for flying and lifelink, making an inexpensive threat relevant deep into the game. Its size shrinks if your life total falls below thirty.

Giada, Font of Hope

A cheap flying, vigilant body and an extra white mana source for your Angel spells, including Akroma. It cannot spend that mana on Vial Smasher or non-Angels. Its counters also make Angels put onto the battlefield by reanimation or Hellkite Courser enter larger.

Knuckles the Echidna

Double strike, trample, and haste make it an excellent independent attacker and a three-keyword contribution to Odric. Connecting with multiple opponents makes Treasure for each player hit, and double strike can trigger again in the second combat-damage step. The thirty-artifact upkeep win is an occasional bonus with Smothering Tithe, not the primary plan.

The Vision

Flying and vigilance provide a solid baseline. Noncreature spells can grant it double strike or indestructible, or draw a card, with each mode available once per turn. Cast the enabling spell before Odric resolves if you want the new ability shared immediately. The temporary abilities disappear at end of turn.

Lyra Dawnbringer

Supplies flying, first strike, and lifelink for Akroma and Odric, while giving your other Angels +1/+1 and lifelink. This improves Akroma herself even though her own trigger cannot. The life recovery pays for Reanimate, Stinging Study, and The One Ring.

Drana and Linvala

A flying, vigilant attacker that shuts off opponents' creature activated abilities, including mana abilities. This can stop opposing engines while your own creatures continue working. It does not suppress triggered abilities, static abilities, or activations from noncreature permanents.

Battle Angels of Tyr

Myriad spreads combat damage across the table, supporting Tymna and your combat-Treasure engines. Its own rewards require the damaged player to be strictly ahead of each other player in the relevant resource; ties give no reward. Myriad copies enter after beginning-of-combat triggers, so they do not inherit the original's temporary Akroma bonus or Odric-granted abilities.

Aurelia, the Law Above

A hasty, flying, vigilant threat that draws when ANY player attacks with at least three creatures. Five attackers also cause three damage to each opponent and gain you three life. Myriad tokens created after declaring attackers do not retroactively increase the number declared for these triggers.

Aurelia, the Warleader

Her first attack untaps your team and creates another combat. Akroma and Odric trigger again at the start of that combat, and Akroma's new bonuses stack with the earlier ones until end of turn. This is one finite extra combat, not an extra turn or an infinite attack loop.

Gisela, Blade of Goldnight

Doubles combat damage and Vial Smasher damage to opponents, and reduces damage aimed at you or your permanents. If casting Gisela is your first spell, Vial's trigger normally resolves before Gisela enters, so that particular trigger is not doubled by her. Gisela needs to already be in play for the multiplier to apply.

Kediss, Emberclaw Familiar

When Akroma or Vial Smasher deals combat damage to one opponent, that commander deals the same amount as noncombat damage to each other opponent. Gisela can multiply both the initial hit and the resulting splash: a six-power unblocked commander deals twelve, then twenty-four to each other opponent. Kediss does not trigger from Vial's spell-driven damage, and its splash does not count as commander damage.

With Akroma, Odric, and Knuckles, the shared set includes flying, first strike, double strike, haste, trample, and vigilance. That gives most of your other creatures +6/+6 for the combat; Vial Smasher gets another +1/+1 for having partner. Add deathtouch and lifelink from Basilisk Collar and Vial can reach eleven power with double strike, enough for twenty-two commander damage if both hits connect. These are board-based finishing combinations that opponents can disrupt.

Equipment that serves the whole team

Basilisk Collar

Gives deathtouch and lifelink for Odric to share and Akroma to count. Equip Vial Smasher to gain life from her noncombat damage too. Deathtouch only changes damage to creatures, not players or planeswalkers, so it does not make a Vial trigger an automatic kill.

Swiftfoot Boots

Gives haste and hexproof, both counted by Akroma and both shareable through Odric. Your own spells can still target the equipped creature, making Boots convenient for Ephemerate and other targeted support.

Lightning Greaves

Free equip supplies haste and protects a key creature, but shroud does not earn an Akroma bonus and stops your own Ephemerate or equipment from targeting it. Move Greaves onto another creature first when necessary; you cannot simply detach them without a legal equip target.

Keeping cards and mana flowing

Esper Sentinel

Draws through opponents' development or makes them spend extra mana. Its tax uses its current power, so Akroma's temporary boost during an opponent's combat can make paying much harder. That boost expires at the end of that opponent's turn rather than accumulating across the whole round.

Tymna the Weaver

Flying, menace, and double strike help you damage multiple opponents and draw up to three cards in the postcombat main phase. Count distinct opponents damaged that turn, not hits or attacking creatures. Kediss's noncombat splash does not increase that count. Tymna's partner ability also gives her one additional Akroma bonus.

The One Ring

A Game Changer that draws without needing the commanders or a safe attack. Casting it gives you a turn of protection, but does not protect your creatures. Burden-counter life loss grows and is not damage, so protection from everything does not stop that upkeep life loss.

Black Market Connections

Choose Treasure when casting Akroma is the bottleneck, cards when you need resources, and a creature when you need another attacker or sacrifice body. The token has all creature types, including Angel, so Lyra boosts it and Giada counts it among Angels already controlled. Creature types are not keywords for Akroma's bonus.

Trouble in Pairs

An independent draw engine that rewards opponents' second spells, second draws, or multi-creature attacks at you. It also stops opponents from starting extra turns. None of that requires your commanders to survive.

Stinging Study

Usually draw seven and lose seven by choosing Akroma in the command zone or on the battlefield. With two commanders, choose ONE eligible commander; you do not add seven and three to draw ten. Casting it as your first spell on an opponent's turn also triggers Vial for five.

Blood for the Blood God!

After enough creatures actually die, this can cost only {B}{B}{R} while retaining mana value eleven. It discards your hand, draws eight, and deals eight to each opponent. With Vial already in play and this as your first spell that turn, add eleven to one randomly chosen opponent; with Gisela already present, those numbers become sixteen to each and twenty-two more to the random opponent. It exiles itself and is expensive without a death-heavy turn.

Archivist of Oghma

Flash provides an opponent-turn spell while opposing searches generate cards and life. It does not need to be cast just to trigger Vial for two; wait until the timing also advances your draw plan or leaves appropriate interaction available.

Smothering Tithe

The second Game Changer supports Akroma's seven-mana cost, expensive follow-ups, and protection held open. Save enough Treasure to interact rather than spending every token to deploy another threat into a potential sweeper.

Professional Face-Breaker

Menace is useful alone and as a keyword for Odric. Each opponent hit in a combat-damage step produces a Treasure; double strike can make another batch. Its sacrifice ability gives temporary access to a card, but you still pay its costs and obey timing rules.

Fable of the Mirror-Breaker // Reflection of Kiki-Jiki

The Goblin makes attack Treasure, the second chapter discards expensive reanimation targets or excess lands, and Reflection later copies useful nonlegendary creatures. Solitude, Fury, Battle Angels of Tyr, Angel of Indemnity, and Hellkite Courser are strong targets. Reflection cannot copy Akroma, either Aurelia, or other legendary creatures.

Arcane Signet

Alongside Fellwar Stone, three Talismans, and three Signets, this supplies the coloured mana a Mardu deck needs. Sol Ring accelerates generic costs but cannot replace the coloured pips in Aurelia or Ruinous Ultimatum. Deploy early rocks even when that means your first Vial trigger is smaller.

Recovering threats and exploiting entry abilities

Liesa, Forgotten Archangel

Returns your other nontoken creatures to hand at the next end step after they die, including evoked Solitude or Fury. It does not return Liesa herself or save creatures exiled directly. Opposing creatures are exiled instead of dying, which denies their graveyard engines but also means those creatures do not reduce Blood for the Blood God's cost.

Reanimate

Recovers a key creature or steals one from an opposing graveyard. Returning Akroma costs seven life and does not count as casting a seven-mana spell: Vial sees the one-mana Reanimate. Leave a commander in the graveyard if you intend to return it this way rather than moving it to the command zone.

Victimize

Exchanges an expendable creature, such as a Goblin or changeling token, for two creatures from your graveyard. They return tapped. Haste does not untap them, so do not assume a returned Aurelia immediately attacks without an additional untap effect.

Angel of Indemnity

Returns any permanent of mana value four or less, including Odric, Vial Smasher, Smothering Tithe, The One Ring, or a land. Encore is a late-game option for several entry triggers and hasty flying attackers. Activating encore is not casting a spell and does not trigger Vial Smasher.

Ephemerate

Blink Solitude or Fury while its evoke-sacrifice trigger is on the stack. The returned creature is a new object, so the old evoke trigger cannot sacrifice it, and you get another entry trigger. Rebound can repeat the blink on your next upkeep, but can also consume that turn's first spell and give Vial only a one-point trigger; casting the rebound is optional.

Hellkite Courser

Puts Akroma directly from the command zone onto the battlefield with haste, bypassing commander tax and supplying a surprise finishing combat. Deploy it before beginning of combat to get Akroma's trigger. Blinking Akroma with Ephemerate before the delayed return resolves leaves the new Akroma permanently; the blink removes the granted haste, so do it after attacking unless another effect restores haste.

Sevinne's Reclamation

Recovers lands, rocks, draw creatures, or other permanents of mana value three or less. Casting from the graveyard with flashback also copies it for another target. Only the cast triggers Vial, not the copy, and its mana value stays three even when you pay five for flashback.

Agadeem's Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt

An early black land when development matters, or a late recovery spell returning creatures with different mana values. With X equal to four, you could return Serra Ascendant, Giada, Tymna, and Odric if all are in your graveyard. On the stack its mana value is X plus three, so a paid large X also creates a substantial first-spell Vial trigger.

Interaction that leaves room for your threats

Swords to Plowshares

Cheap exile for the creature that would stop your attack or win the game. Path to Exile is the second one-mana version, at the cost of giving its controller a basic land. Do not postpone a necessary answer just to engineer a larger Vial trigger.

Anguished Unmaking

Exiles a problematic nonland permanent at instant speed, including graveyard hate that would prevent your recovery. Its life payment is manageable with the deck's lifelink, but still counts against the life available for Study and Reanimate.

Generous Gift

Broad permanent removal when narrower answers do not fit. Chaos Warp provides another broad answer with a different replacement risk. Neither is reserved for creature removal; opposing engines often deserve attention first.

Deadly Rollick

Exiles a creature for no mana with either commander on the battlefield, while still having mana value four for Vial. Its free-cast condition does not require both commanders or Akroma specifically.

Snuff Out

Four life can replace the mana cost if you control a Swamp, including a typed original dual or shockland. It still has mana value four. It cannot target black creatures and destruction does not beat indestructible.

Solitude

Flash removal that can be cast on an opponent's turn for a five-point Vial trigger even when evoked. Pitching a white card costs a real resource. Pair it with Ephemerate or Liesa when possible to retain or recover the creature, and use its lifelink as an Odric contribution when it stays in play.

Fury

Evoke provides a free multi-target removal option while retaining mana value five; double strike makes the full creature a serious Akroma payoff. Unlike Solitude it has no flash, so ordinary creature timing applies. You cannot divide less than one damage to any chosen target.

Reprieve

Returns a spell to its owner's hand and replaces itself, buying a key turn even against a spell that cannot be countered. The opponent can cast it again when they have the mana and timing, so use the window to advance your plan or finish the game.

Witch Enchanter // Witch-Blessed Meadow

Artifact or enchantment removal attached to a creature that can be blinked or reanimated, with a white-land reverse side for early consistency. Playing it as a land does not cast a spell or trigger Vial Smasher.

Blasphemous Act

Often costs only {R} on a crowded battlefield but has mana value nine. If it is your first spell with Vial present, the nine-damage trigger resolves before the wipe. Protect your team with Boros Charm or another suitable effect when that creates an advantageous reset. It normally kills Vial too if you do not protect her.

Ruinous Ultimatum

Destroys opponents' nonland permanents while preserving your board, opening a decisive attack. The mana base and Treasures must support {R}{R}{W}{W}{W}{B}{B}; Sol Ring alone does not help pay that cost. Indestructible opposing permanents survive it.

Preserving a winning board

Teferi's Protection

The third Game Changer protects your life total and phases out all your permanents through the next turn boundary. It covers situations indestructible does not, including many exile wipes. The spell itself exiles after resolving, so the deck does not recur it in a loop.

Clever Concealment

Convoke helps protect any number of your nonland permanents by phasing them out. Tapping creatures to convoke can cost an attack if they are not phased out and later returned untapped in your untap step. If you phase Vial out while her damage trigger is still on the stack, that trigger remains independent and can resolve.

Boros Charm

Most often protects all your permanents from destruction, or supplies double strike for a lethal commander hit. Giving indestructible before Odric and Akroma resolve can also improve the shared keyword package. Four damage to a player is occasionally the cleanest way to finish someone.

Deflecting Swat

Free with either commander, redirects targeted removal or certain counterspells where legal new targets exist. It does not answer an untargeted wipe. Vial Smasher's random-damage ability itself does not target, so redirection based on changing targets cannot aim it for you.

Bolt Bend

A second redirection effect that often costs only {R} when Akroma or another four-power creature is present, while retaining mana value four. The power discount is checked while casting; do not assume a creature will receive Akroma's combat boost later in time to pay less now.

Mana choices and backup utility

Vault of the Archangel

Grants your entire team deathtouch and lifelink. Activate before Akroma's trigger resolves to count both abilities, or in response to a Vial trigger to gain life from its damage. The activation costs four mana plus tapping the Vault, so budget five lands' worth of resources when it is your only mana source for that job.

Cavern of Souls

Usually name Angel to secure Akroma and the Angel threats. Its coloured mana can only cast creatures of the chosen type; the unrestricted colourless ability can help other spells. Fetchlands, original duals, shocks, and multiplayer duals provide the rest of the three-colour fixing.

Takenuma, Abandoned Mire

Recovers a key creature without spending a spell slot; channel is an activated ability, so it does not trigger Vial. Eiganjo, Seat of the Empire supplies another land-slot answer, while Minas Tirith draws after an attack with at least two creatures. Use these as lands first when casting Akroma on time depends on it.

What to keep in an opening hand and how to sequence

Keep three lands with access to all three colours, an early rock or Giada, and a useful draw creature or interaction spell. Two-land hands need especially good cheap development and a credible route to the third land. Mulligan hands with several expensive Angels and no ramp; Akroma already guarantees access to a late threat.

An example keep is Command Tower, Marsh Flats, Sacred Foundry, Arcane Signet, Esper Sentinel, Knuckles the Echidna, and Swords to Plowshares. Fetch Scrubland or Godless Shrine when black is missing, then prioritize the mix of early cards, development, and protection demanded by the table. Do not spend the removal merely to generate a small Vial trigger.

Vial normally enters before Akroma, making subsequent development and opponent-turn interaction chip away at life totals. Akroma should arrive when you have creatures worth amplifying or a protected finishing line. She is not an efficient seven-mana play into an empty board unless the game specifically calls for her defensive body.

Before combat, equip keyword sources and decide whether The Vision needs an enabling noncreature spell. Stack Akroma first and Odric second so Odric resolves first. After that calculation, adding a new keyword does not retroactively increase the bonus already granted, though the new ability still works normally.

For an Aurelia turn, account for the second Akroma trigger and the fact that the first bonus lasts until end of turn. Spread evasive attacks to generate cards and Treasure, then concentrate damage when a player can be eliminated. Kediss splash and Vial damage are normal life-total pressure; track combat commander damage separately for Akroma and Vial rather than combining them.

Against graveyard hate, hard-cast threats and preserve the independent draw engines. Against sweepers, keep one recovery route or a protection spell rather than deploying every creature. Liesa helps against death, not exile, and a reanimated threat does not provide the same Vial damage as casting that threat.

The three Game Changers are Smothering Tithe, The One Ring, and Teferi's Protection. The deck aims for strong Bracket 3 combat finishes backed by interaction and accumulated resources, with no planned infinite combo, mass land denial, or extra-turn chain. Aurelia supplies a finite extra combat; the list does not contain a repeatable attack loop.
