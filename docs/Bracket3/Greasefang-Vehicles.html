Put the Vehicle into your graveyard before your beginning-of-combat trigger needs a target, and decide how it will get back there after attacking. Greasefang supplies haste, not automatic animation: you must still crew the Vehicle before declaring attackers. A newly cast Greasefang can crew immediately because crew taps creatures as a cost of the Vehicle's ability, not their own tap ability.

This is a Bracket 3 Vehicle-reanimator deck with three Game Changers: Smothering Tithe, The One Ring, and Teferi's Protection. Its twelve Vehicles supply damage, removal, cards, and lasting bodies. There is no planned infinite combo, mass land denial, or extra-turn chain. It builds pressure early and wins through established combat resources, with protection and recovery for longer games.

Make the first combat useful

Greasefang, Okiba Boss

A three-mana 4/3 that can crew most of the deck's Vehicles alone. Its trigger targets a Vehicle already in your graveyard as the trigger is put on the stack. Discarding your first Vehicle after that point is too late for that combat. Once the trigger is on the stack, removing Greasefang does not counter it, but may leave you without enough creatures to crew.

Putrid Imp

A one-mana, repeatable discard outlet that needs neither mana nor tapping to activate. Discard the intended Vehicle before combat, and discard it again after the end-step return when no sacrifice outlet is available. Flying is a small benefit; setting up the graveyard is its main job.

Tireless Tribe

A second one-mana outlet that can discard repeatedly without tapping. Its toughness boost helps it block, but does not increase its power for crew. Do not treat discarding a card to Tribe as adding four crew power.

The Underworld Cookbook

Discards a Vehicle while making a Food to sacrifice or cash in later. It can tap immediately because it is not a creature. Its recovery ability returns a creature card to hand, which can retrieve Greasefang if you left it in the graveyard, but cannot retrieve an ordinary Vehicle card.

Key to the City

Discards a card with up to one creature target, so you can activate it with no target to set up Greasefang. If a Vehicle is already a creature, the unblockability helps it connect. A returned Vehicle in the graveyard is not a legal target, and using Key before combat to discard it does not also make that Vehicle unblockable after it returns.

The Queen of Dale

Each opponent's first noncreature spell each turn lets you draw and discard, with a Soldier if the discarded card is nonland. That can put a Vehicle into your graveyard while producing crew material. Unlike a free activated outlet, this depends on opponents supplying the trigger.

Ripples of Undeath

Mills three before combat and can recover a useful milled card for mana and life. Usually leave a Vehicle in the graveyard and retrieve a land, protection spell, or draw engine. You cannot choose a card that was already in the graveyard before that trigger's milling.

Entomb

Finds the exact Vehicle required for the next combat: Parhelion II for an army, Demonic Junker for opposing creatures, Nautiloid Ship for a graveyard, or another utility Vehicle. It puts the card in your graveyard rather than your hand, so it needs Greasefang or an appropriate recovery engine to matter immediately.

Unmarked Grave

Provides another cheap graveyard tutor, but only for nonlegendary cards. It can find Reaver Titan, Thunderhawk Gunship, Demonic Junker, or Salvation Engine. It cannot find Parhelion II or Skysovereign, Consul Flagship.

Cynical Loner

Crew a Vehicle with it to tap it safely; at the beginning of your second main phase, its survival trigger can put any library card into your graveyard. This normally sets up your next combat, not the combat that just ended. Summoning sickness does not prevent it from helping crew.

Hotshot Mechanic

A one-mana creature that crews as though it had four power, independently handling Parhelion II or Reaver Titan. It is also an artifact for your artifact-count synergies. It cannot crew Salvation Engine alone, which needs six total crew power.

Choose the Vehicle for the actual board

Parhelion II

The main army-building threat. Greasefang crews it alone, and attacking creates two 4/4 flying vigilant Angels that remain after the Vehicle leaves. The Vehicle plus its new Angels represent thirteen damage if all connect. You may send the new Angels at different opponents; they enter attacking and do not separately trigger abilities that require attackers to be declared.

Reaver Titan

Its attack trigger deals five to each opponent before combat damage, pressuring the whole table while its 10/10 body attacks one defender. Protection from mana value three or less stops many targeted answers and blockers. Crew does not target, so Greasefang can crew it despite that protection. It does not have trample or flying.

Thunderhawk Gunship

Makes two vigilant 2/2 creatures on entry, so it provides its own crew material immediately. Those newly created tokens can crew despite summoning sickness. Its attack trigger gives flying to your attacking creatures, helping the army left by earlier turns connect.

Skysovereign, Consul Flagship

Removes small creatures or planeswalkers on entry and again on attack. Its damage does not target players. Recurring it repeatedly controls opposing utility creatures while applying six flying combat damage.

Demonic Junker

On entry, can destroy up to one creature for each player, including an optional creature of yours. You do not need to destroy your own creature to destroy opponents' creatures; doing so merely earns the two counters. Its affinity makes it a reasonable hardcast later if Greasefang is unavailable.

Salvation Engine

Recovers another artifact on attack and pumps your other artifact creatures. The recovered artifact is not subject to Greasefang's return-to-hand instruction. A returned Vehicle does not gain haste from Salvation Engine, but entry abilities work immediately. Crew six requires extra help beyond Greasefang alone.

Nautiloid Ship

Exiles one player's graveyard on entry and can steal a creature from the cards that particular Ship exiled when it connects. A later blink or reanimation makes it a new object that cannot access the old object's exiled cards. Use it as graveyard interaction first and a theft threat when the board allows.

N'Yami-Class Mother Ship

An evasive, vigilant threat that converts combat damage into either a permanent from the top of your library entering or a card in your hand. A land put onto the battlefield this way does not use your normal land play. It already has haste when hardcast.

The Thanos-Copter

Animates the Vehicles you already control when its entry trigger resolves, including itself, and draws when your Vehicles deal combat damage to players. Its animation does not grant haste to the other Vehicles or include Vehicles that enter later. The Copter itself has haste even when hardcast.

The Regalia

Ramps a land into play tapped on every attack and only needs crew one. It is a useful castable threat when graveyard access is unavailable. Its reveal sequence puts the nonland cards on the bottom, not into your graveyard.

Smuggler's Copter

A cheap flying attacker and blocker that loots, giving the deck useful development before the large Vehicles arrive. Its attack loot happens after Greasefang has already triggered, so a Vehicle discarded then usually waits for next turn.

Valor's Flagship

A large flying lifelink threat that helps recover life spent on draw and lands. Cycling puts it directly into the graveyard and replaces it in hand. Cycling with X equal to zero costs three mana and makes no Pilots; paying more creates Pilots that can help crew future Vehicles.

Keep the Vehicles working after combat

Arcbound Ravager

A free artifact-sacrifice outlet that puts a borrowed Vehicle back into the graveyard before the end-step return. It can sacrifice an uncrewed Vehicle too. Its modular death trigger needs an artifact creature target, so an ordinary uncrewed Vehicle cannot receive the counters.

Dockside Chef

Trades a borrowed Vehicle for a card while setting up next turn's reanimation. It does not require the Vehicle to be a creature and does not require Chef to tap, but you must reserve the two mana for the activation.

Deadly Dispute

Sacrifices a Vehicle as an additional cost to draw two and make a Treasure. Cast it after combat damage, or in response to exile removal when the Vehicle is more useful in your graveyard. If the spell is countered, the sacrifice still happened.

Morbid Curiosity

Converts a high-mana-value Vehicle into a large refill during your second main phase. Sacrificing Parhelion II draws eight cards, regardless of whether it cost you mana to reanimate it. It is a sorcery, so do not wait until the end-step return trigger is on the stack.

Braids, Arisen Nightmare

At your end step, put Greasefang's return trigger on the stack first and Braids above it. Sacrifice the Vehicle to Braids before it returns to hand. Opponents can sacrifice a matching type or let you draw and lose two life; three cards are not guaranteed. A crewed Vehicle is both an artifact and a creature, giving opponents more matching options.

Phyrexian Tower

Sacrifices a crewed Vehicle for two black mana, avoiding the bounce and helping cast a second-main-phase spell. A Vehicle must currently be a creature to pay that cost. Crew lasts through the end step, but mana made there will not carry into your next turn.

Phyrexia's Core

Sacrifices any artifact, including an uncrewed Vehicle, for one mana and its tap. The life is minor; the important function is putting the Vehicle back where Greasefang can use it.

Teleportation Circle

Stack its end-step blink above Greasefang's return trigger. The Vehicle leaves and reenters as a new object, so the old return instruction cannot find it. You keep the Vehicle and get its entry ability again, but it loses temporary animation and haste from the old object. Next turn, it must be crewed normally.

Touch the Spirit Realm

Channel it after combat to blink the borrowed Vehicle until the next end step. The returning Vehicle is a new object and avoids Greasefang's old return instruction. Channel before the end step begins if you want it back that same end step; channeling after the step has begun delays the return until a later end step. Its enchantment mode also answers an opposing artifact or creature.

When you keep a Vehicle permanently, Greasefang needs a different Vehicle in the graveyard next turn. Sometimes sacrificing Parhelion II is better than blinking it because you want the next attack to receive haste and leave your mana available for protection.

Turn recursion and discard into cards and bodies

Imotekh the Stormlord

Creates two Necron artifact creatures whenever one or more artifact cards leave your graveyard. Greasefang and Salvation Engine supply separate opportunities. Those tokens can crew immediately. Its beginning-of-combat boost must target an artifact creature already on the battlefield when that trigger goes on the stack; it cannot target the Vehicle Greasefang is about to return.

Monument to Endurance

Rewards discarding with a card, Treasure, or three life loss to every opponent, with each mode usable once per turn. Putrid Imp or Tireless Tribe can trigger it on opposing turns too. The Vehicle returning to hand is not itself a discard; you must actually discard a card to earn a trigger.

Black Market Connections

Supplies cards, Treasures, and optional crew bodies before combat. Taking all three modes costs six life, so select modes according to the board. The changeling token has three power and can crew many Vehicles, but cannot crew Parhelion II alone.

The One Ring

An independent draw engine that buys a turn of protection when cast. Its burden counters eventually demand a life-management plan. Sacrifice it to Ravager, Chef, Braids, Dispute, or Curiosity if necessary; indestructible does not stop sacrifice. Salvation Engine can later return it, but returning it without casting does not grant the protection trigger.

Esper Sentinel

Early draw pressure and a small body to help crew or supplement Greasefang for Salvation Engine. Do not sacrifice it for minor value while opponents are still paying you cards every round.

Skullclamp

Turns one-toughness Soldiers, Warriors, or other expendable creatures into two cards. Larger Angels and crewed Vehicles do not die merely from equipping it, but draw cards if subsequently sacrificed while equipped. It cannot be equipped to an uncrewed Vehicle.

Spend your mana on development and a protected attack

Sol Ring

Accelerates artifacts and the generic parts of your spells, but Greasefang still needs white and black. A hand with Ring and only colorless lands is not automatically keepable.

Talisman of Hierarchy

An untapped two-mana accelerator that supplies either commander color. Arcane Signet, Orzhov Signet, and Fellwar Stone offer redundancy; the Signet needs mana to activate, while Fellwar Stone depends on opponents' lands for its colors.

Liquimetal Torque

Helps cast your Vehicles, and can temporarily turn an opposing nonland permanent into an artifact for Loran of the Third Path or Witch Enchanter to destroy. It cannot tap for mana and use its conversion ability at the same time.

Smothering Tithe

Supplies mana for hardcasting Vehicles and holding up interaction while the graveyard engine operates. Its Treasures also increase artifact counts and can feed Ravager or other sacrifice effects. Opponents can pay, so do not base an opening hand entirely on guaranteed Treasure income.

Akroma's Will

With Greasefang present as you cast it, choose both modes to give your creatures flying, vigilance, double strike, lifelink, indestructible, and protection from all colors. Crew before resolving it so the Vehicles receive those effects. To include the Angels from the current Parhelion attack, cast it after the attack trigger has resolved and created them. First strike plus double strike does not create a third combat-damage step.

Grand Abolisher

Protects your combat and recovery plays from opposing spells and activated abilities of their artifacts, creatures, and enchantments during your turn. Land abilities and triggered abilities remain available, including some forms of graveyard interaction.

Voice of Victory

Stops opposing spells during your turn, though activated abilities remain usable. Its two attacking Warriors add pressure and can feed your sacrifice outlets after combat. They enter too late to crew a Vehicle for that same declaration of attackers.

Swiftfoot Boots

Protects Greasefang from targeted removal while still letting your own spells target it. Its haste is useful for attacking with utility creatures but is not required for a newly cast Greasefang to crew a Vehicle. Equipping uses sorcery timing.

Teferi's Protection

Protects your board, life total, and graveyard-independent resources for a turn cycle. Phasing a borrowed Vehicle out before its end-step return resolves also lets it avoid that one-shot return instruction. It phases in during your next untap step, while its haste and crew effects from the previous turn have expired normally.

Clever Concealment

Protects selected nonland engines and threats through phasing, with crew bodies or tokens helping pay convoke. It can also make the borrowed Vehicle unavailable to the return trigger. Creatures already tapped to crew cannot also tap to convoke, and colorless creatures cannot pay the white symbols.

Flawless Maneuver

Protects creatures from destruction for free with Greasefang present. Uncrewed Vehicles are not creatures and do not receive the effect, so crew any Vehicle you need protected before it resolves. It does not stop exile, sacrifice, or lethal toughness reduction.

Malakir Rebirth // Malakir Mire

Protects Greasefang from dying or serves as a tapped black land early. If relying on the granted death trigger, leave Greasefang in the graveyard instead of moving it to the command zone. It can also return a crewed Vehicle that dies, because the granted trigger existed before death; the returned Vehicle is a new, normally uncrewed permanent without Greasefang's old bounce instruction.

Answer the obstacle and recover your engine

Bitter Triumph

Removes a creature or planeswalker while discarding the Vehicle you wanted in the graveyard. The alternative life payment is useful when no card should be discarded. Save it for a real obstacle rather than spending removal just to enable a discard.

Anguished Unmaking

Exiles a problematic nonland permanent, including graveyard hate or an indestructible threat. Generous Gift covers any permanent through destruction, while Swords to Plowshares and Path to Exile are efficient creature answers. Be ready to remove graveyard hate before committing a combat to recursion.

Damn

Functions as targeted creature removal or a four-mana creature wipe through overload. Uncrewed Vehicles survive the creature wipe, but Greasefang and your tokens do not normally survive. Casting it before you crew preserves more of your artifact board.

Toxic Deluge

Controls the size of a creature reset and handles indestructibility with toughness reduction. It affects your own creatures too, and Flawless Maneuver cannot save them from lethal -X/-X. Vehicles that remain noncreatures when the spell resolves are unaffected.

Soul-Guide Lantern

Provides immediate graveyard interaction before Nautiloid Ship is available. Its mass-exile activation clears only opposing graveyards, leaving your Vehicles for Greasefang. Draw a card instead only when you can afford to give up the graveyard answer.

Reanimate

Retrieves Greasefang or a useful support creature for one mana and life equal to its mana value. Vehicle cards in graveyards are not normally creature cards, even if they were crewed before dying, so this spell does not directly reanimate the Vehicle package.

Sevinne's Reclamation

Retrieves Greasefang, a cheap outlet, an engine such as Monument to Endurance, or a land. Flashback provides a second target through its copy. It checks the card's mana value, so it cannot return Parhelion II merely because Greasefang can return it cheaply.

Lively Dirge

Costs three mana for its graveyard-tutor mode, four for its creature-return mode, or five for both. With Greasefang already in your graveyard, both modes can put a Vehicle into the graveyard and return Greasefang plus a one-mana creature, ready for combat. The returned creatures' total mana value cannot exceed four.

Agadeem's Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt

A black land when needed or recovery for several support creatures later. Returned creatures must have different mana values, each no greater than X. Like Reanimate, it does not return ordinary Vehicle cards from the graveyard.

Urza's Saga

Produces crew-capable Constructs and finds Sol Ring, Skullclamp, The Underworld Cookbook, or Soul-Guide Lantern with its third chapter. That chapter checks an exact printed mana cost of zero or one generic, so it cannot find Hotshot Mechanic, whose cost is white. Saga eventually sacrifices itself; count permanent colored mana sources separately.

Mech Hangar

Can produce either commander color because Greasefang is a Pilot. That colored mana cannot pay for unrelated spells or abilities. Its three-mana animation ability gives a Vehicle another way to become a creature, but grants no haste, so a newly hardcast Vehicle without haste still cannot attack immediately.

What to keep and how to sequence

Keep three functional mana sources, a discard or graveyard-tutor effect, and either a Vehicle or a way to find one. A two-land hand needs a credible route to the third land; cheap discard alone does not make it keepable. The deck has 35 lands plus Malakir Rebirth, Agadeem's Awakening, and Witch Enchanter as three land options. Fetchlands find Scrubland, Godless Shrine, or Shadowy Backstreet for both colors; only the surveil land always enters tapped.

An example keep is Scrubland, Marsh Flats, Command Tower, Putrid Imp, Parhelion II, Arcane Signet, and Bitter Triumph. Develop the Imp, then the Signet if the board permits. Discard Parhelion before combat on the turn you cast Greasefang, return it, and crew it with Greasefang before declaring attackers. Preserve Bitter Triumph for an obstacle; the Imp already handles the discard.

Use that first attack to establish resources, then plan the second: find an outlet, recover protection, or shift to a utility Vehicle when the table threatens to win. Do not sacrifice a Vehicle before its attack or combat-damage ability has done the job you needed. Mana spent on an outlet after combat should be reserved before you cast extra spells.

Most early Vehicles are best crewed by Greasefang, leaving smaller creatures untapped for other jobs. For Salvation Engine, count the full six crew power. Creatures can crew despite summoning sickness, and a Vehicle made into a creature can help crew another Vehicle if it is untapped and has sufficient power; do not tap the attacker you intended to use unless another threat is worth more.

If opponents repeatedly remove Greasefang, use The Regalia, Smuggler's Copter, your mana engines, and hardcast threats to keep developing. Sevinne's Reclamation, Reanimate, and Lively Dirge offer recovery when you can safely leave Greasefang in the graveyard. Salvation Engine provides artifact recursion without the commander. This backup plan is slower, and graveyard exile can still force you into ordinary casting.

Before a finishing combat, count available crew, flying or other evasion, attack-trigger damage, and protection. Use Akroma's Will after the relevant Vehicles are creatures and after Parhelion's new Angels exist if you need them doubled too. Pressure the whole table with durable tokens and Reaver Titan rather than assuming one large Vehicle can efficiently eliminate three opponents by itself.
