Plain text ↗ On GitHub ↗

Blitz is an alternative casting cost, not a way to put creatures directly into play. It supplies haste, a draw trigger when that creature dies, and a delayed sacrifice. Attack first, then turn the creature you were going to lose into mana, cards, another creature, or direct damage. A creature returned from the graveyard is a new object and is no longer blitzed.

This Bracket 3 build uses 39 creatures including Henzie, 35 lands plus two modal spell/land cards, and three Game Changers: Survival of the Fittest, Demonic Tutor, and Gaea's Cradle. The plan is accelerating into efficient blitz threats and recycling their strongest triggers. Its wins are combat, drain, and mass reanimation rather than a compact infinite combo. A-tier reflects that resilient engine and its explosive finishing turns, not an unrestricted combo build.

Get Henzie working early
Henzie "Toolbox" Torre

Usually aim for turn two off a colored accelerator, then blitz a four- or five-mana value creature. Henzie grants blitz only to creature spells with mana value at least four. His first command-zone cast already gives a one-generic reduction; later command-zone casts increase it. Reanimation does not increase that count, and reductions cannot remove colored pips.

++
Birds of Paradise + Ignoble Hierarch + Delighted Halfling

The best one-mana development supplies the colors Henzie actually needs. Halfling's colored mana is restricted to legendary spells and makes them uncounterable, so its colorless mode may be required for a nonlegendary blitz creature.

+++
Llanowar Elves + Elvish Mystic + Fyndhorn Elves + Elves of Deep Shadow

The extra one-mana creatures increase the chance of turn-two Henzie. Plan the lands around the color the accelerator supplies. Later they become sacrifice fodder, mana for Cradle, or attackers under Pathbreaker Ibex.

Deathrite Shaman

Nine fetchlands make early land cards available for its mana ability, but it is not guaranteed acceleration without one in a graveyard. Later activations can remove a recursion target or drain opponents. Its targeted mana ability uses the stack, so a graveyard response can prevent the mana.

+
Utopia Sprawl + Wild Growth

One-mana acceleration that survives most creature wipes. Sprawl requires a Forest, which includes the typed duals, and should name the missing color for Henzie. Losing the enchanted land loses the Aura too.

+
Nature's Lore + Three Visits

Search Forest duals to fix colors and accelerate while preserving creatures. An original dual enters untapped; a shockland requires the usual life payment. Neither is restricted to basic Forests.

+
Bloom Tender + Henzie "Toolbox" Torre

With Henzie in play, Tender supplies black, red, and green together. It needs to survive summoning sickness before using that ability; it does not have haste just because Henzie is on the battlefield.

+
Sol Ring + Arcane Signet

Ring accelerates generic costs but cannot pay any of Henzie's three colored symbols. Signet fixes all three. The opening hand still needs colored lands even when it contains Ring.

+
Gaea's Cradle + Phyrexian Tower

Cradle rewards the early creature board; use its mana before sacrificing your creatures. Tower converts a spent blitz creature into two black mana while still allowing its death draw. Neither is a reliable colored source in an empty opening board.

Blitz creatures that leave something behind
Aerid Konstrari

With Henzie's first discount, blitz costs red-green-green. It creates an untapped Heartwood on entry and another on death, leaving two permanent mana artifacts after attacking and drawing from blitz. Its fixed colored cost limits later discounts, but the lasting ramp is excellent.

Overlord of the Hauntwoods

Blitz gives an entry trigger and an immediate attack trigger, creating two tapped lands that fix all colors. Blitz and impending are alternative costs: you cannot use both for the same cast. The Everywhere tokens remain after the creature dies.

Blossoming Tortoise

Entry and attack each mill three and can return a land from your graveyard tapped. A fetchland can supply a target before the first trigger. This develops mana and reanimation material; it does not guarantee two lands if the graveyard and mills contain none.

Roxanne, Starfall Savant

Entry and attack each create a tapped Meteorite and its two-damage trigger. Those artifacts remain after blitz. While Roxanne survives, Meteorites, Heartwoods, and Treasures produce an extra mana when tapped for mana; the bonus disappears when she leaves.

Atsushi, the Blazing Sky

Choose three Treasures after attacking to fund the next turn, or two exiled cards playable through the end of your next turn when mana is already plentiful. That permission does not grant extra land plays or change normal spell timing.

Ojer Kaslem, Deepest Growth // Temple of Cultivation

Haste lets it attack and potentially turn combat damage into both a creature and a land. Blitz death still draws and returns Ojer as a tapped land. The transformed land is a new object and is not sacrificed again by the old blitz effect.

Vaultborn Tyrant

Entry gains life and draws, large later creatures do the same, and its nontoken death leaves a permanent token copy behind. That copy was not blitzed and does not inherit the granted haste or blitz draw ability. It cannot make another copy when it dies because it is a token.

Recycle the best triggers
Survival of the Fittest

Discard an expensive creature you intend to reanimate to find the creature the current board requires. This turns spare mana creatures into threats and stocks the graveyard, but it costs a creature card and green mana per activation. Keep protection for it if it is your only engine.

Meren of Clan Nel Toth

Usually cast normally so she remains for repeated end steps. Blitz deaths build experience; low experience returns a target to hand, while enough experience returns it to the battlefield. Her end-step target must already be in the graveyard when the trigger goes on the stack: sacrificing a creature later in that same end step cannot make it her target retroactively.

Chainer, Nightmare Adept

Discard to cast one creature from your graveyard that turn, paying its costs normally or using Henzie's blitz permission when eligible. His other ability grants haste to nontoken creatures that entered without being cast from your hand, including reanimation. The activation is once per turn and does not let ordinary creatures be cast at instant speed.

+
Honest Rutstein + Eternal Witness

Rutstein returns a creature to hand and discounts future creature spells, including their blitz costs. Witness recovers any spent card, especially protection or Living Death. Neither has enough mana value to gain blitz from Henzie.

+
Reanimate + Animate Dead

Cheap ways to keep a major creature permanently rather than losing it to blitz. Reanimate costs life equal to mana value; Animate Dead's creature depends on the Aura remaining attached and on the battlefield. Both can use opposing graveyards, and neither satisfies a creature's requirement that you cast it.

Junji, the Midnight Sky

Its death can reanimate a non-Dragon creature from any graveyard for two life, or pressure every opponent's hand and life total. Junji cannot return itself, Kokusho, or your other Dragons. Archon, Avacyn, and Etali are valid creature types for its reanimation mode.

Avacyn, Angel of Horror

Deaths of your nontoken creatures, including Avacyn herself, set up returns at the beginning of the next end step. Sacrifice a blitz creature before your end step begins to get it back that turn. If it dies to blitz during the end step, its return waits until the next player's end step. Returned creatures are not blitzed; cards that changed zones again are not returned by the old delayed trigger.

Riveteers Ascendancy

A sacrifice can return a creature with strictly lower mana value tapped, once each turn. Recover a utility creature while cashing in a large blitz threat. The sacrificed card cannot target itself, and combat deaths do not trigger an ability that specifically requires sacrifice.

Victimize

Choose two creatures already in your graveyard, then sacrifice a creature on resolution to return both tapped. A creature sacrificed to pay this exchange cannot be one of the original targets. This is especially efficient with a spent blitz body and two expensive threats.

Cash in creatures before the automatic sacrifice
Greater Good

Sacrifice an attacker after damage to draw its current power, then discard three. The blitz death draw is another trigger and resolves separately. The discarded cards set up reanimation; sacrificing a tiny creature can be a poor card exchange even though the activation is free.

+
Viscera Seer + Phyrexian Tower

Sacrifice outlets let you dodge exile removal, put creatures into the graveyard before Living Death, or arrange Avacyn's timing. Seer is repeatable and scries; Tower makes mana but must tap. Sacrificing a creature cannot undo an entry trigger already on the stack.

Skullclamp

Equip a blitz attacker so its death draws two additional cards. On a 1-toughness creature the -1 toughness kills it immediately, providing cards but sacrificing that mana source. Henzie draws only for creatures actually cast using blitz, not every creature that dies.

The Great Henge

Cast it while a large blitz creature is still on the battlefield for the discount. It then adds mana, life, counters, and a card for each subsequent nontoken creature entry. The draw does not require casting, so reanimation also triggers it.

Disciple of Freyalise // Garden of Freyalise

A land when the hand needs one; later a creature that converts another creature's power into life and cards. The three green symbols are not reduced by blitz. Sacrifice a blitz attacker after combat to get its death draw as well as Disciple's payoff.

Birthing Pod

After attacking, sacrifice a blitz creature in your second main phase to fetch a creature with mana value exactly one higher. The sacrificed creature's printed mana value matters, not what you paid. The fetched creature is not blitzed and normally cannot attack that turn. Pod is sorcery-speed and only taps once without another untap effect.

Birthing Ritual

Put its end-step trigger above the blitz sacrifice triggers so it resolves first. Look at seven before deciding whether to sacrifice, then take a creature with mana value at most one more than the sacrificed creature. This is selection, not a guaranteed tutor; you may decline the sacrifice if the revealed cards are poor.

Ziatora, the Incinerator

Put Ziatora's trigger above the automatic blitz sacrifices. She can sacrifice another creature to deal its power as damage and create three Treasures, while that creature still draws from blitz. Ziatora's damage can remove a blocker or finish a player. She cannot sacrifice herself to her own ability.

Turn accumulated value into a win
Pathbreaker Ibex

Blitz supplies the haste it needs to attack immediately. Its trigger gives your entire team trample and a bonus equal to your greatest creature power as the trigger resolves. Big reanimated creatures turn even the early mana creatures into lethal attackers; a mostly empty board gives a much smaller result.

Archon of Cruelty

Entry and attack each pressure one targeted opponent while gaining you life and cards. Aim the triggers at the player whose blockers, cards, or life total matter most. Reanimation repeats its entry effect, but each trigger targets one opponent rather than all three.

Etali, Primal Conqueror // Etali, Primal Sickness

The entry effect casts a nonland card from each player's library for free, then blitz lets Etali attack. Transforming it is a late poison-based finish, not a reason to keep a slow hand. Transforming a blitzed Etali does not make it a new object, so its pending sacrifice still applies.

+
Terror of the Peaks + Living Death

Returning a group of large creatures alongside Terror produces a damage trigger for each other creature entering under your control, including those returning simultaneously. Assign damage after evaluating opposing entry triggers and possible protection. This can finish multiple opponents without combat but is not automatically lethal with any graveyard.

+
Kokusho, the Evening Star + Gray Merchant of Asphodel

Kokusho's death drains every opponent for five; Merchant drains based on your black devotion when its entry trigger resolves. These also restore life spent on reanimation and lands. Keeping your black permanents until Merchant resolves improves the drain.

+
Living Death + Soul-Guide Lantern

Exile opponents' graveyards before resolving Living Death to make the mass exchange much more favorable. Sacrifice your desired creatures before casting or resolving Living Death so they are among the cards exiled from your graveyard and returned. Creatures sacrificed during the spell itself are not the ones it returns. Opponents can respond to the Lantern activation.

Bringer of the Last Gift

Its mass-reanimation entry effect works only if you cast it; blitz qualifies, ordinary reanimation does not. Previously dead creatures return, but the creatures sacrificed by this entry effect stay in their graveyards. It affects all players, so inspect opposing graveyards and use Lantern when necessary.

+
Chainer, Nightmare Adept + Living Death

If Chainer returns alongside your other nontoken creatures, he sees them enter and grants haste because they were not cast from your hand. This can make the rebuilt board attack immediately in your main phase. Creatures returned by Victimize still enter tapped, so haste alone does not let them attack that turn.

Answers that advance the creature plan
Druid of Purification

Removes several opposing artifacts or enchantments while providing a blitz body and death draw. Every chosen permanent must be one YOU do not control, even when an opponent makes the choice. Opponents may decline to help, so only your own choice is guaranteed.

+
Ravenous Chupacabra + Noxious Gearhulk

Repeatable removal attached to creatures you can blitz, sacrifice, or reanimate. Chupacabra is the efficient early play; Gearhulk adds a larger attacker and life gain. Destroy effects do not remove indestructible creatures.

Bane of Progress

A powerful emergency reset that also destroys your own artifacts and enchantments, including your mana tokens, Pod, Henge, and reanimation Aura. Use it when the opposing board is worth that cost, or before deploying your own engines. Its counters can make it a huge blitz attacker.

Massacre Wurm

Entry clears small opposing creatures and its life-loss ability punishes their deaths while it is present. Reusing it can dismantle token boards without harming your creatures. Larger creatures may survive the -2/-2.

++
Assassin's Trophy + Abrupt Decay + Deadly Rollick

Efficient answers supplement the creatures: Trophy handles any opposing permanent, Decay handles a cheap nonland permanent without being countered, and Rollick exiles a creature. Rollick is free only with a commander actually on your battlefield. Trophy may give its controller a basic land.

++
Deflecting Swat + Veil of Summer + Heroic Intervention

Swat redirects targeted interaction and is free with Henzie present. Veil protects your spells from countering and your side from blue or black targeting, but not all removal colors. Intervention stops destruction and opposing targeting, but not untargeted exile, sacrifice, or toughness reduction. It does not stop blitz's sacrifice.

Malakir Rebirth // Malakir Mire

Protect a useful creature from destruction or deliberately use it before a blitz sacrifice to return the creature tapped as a new object. You still draw for its original blitz death. The return does not immediately attack, and the spell does not answer exile. Use its land face when needed to keep development moving.

Tutor for the board you face
→
Survival of the Fittest → Aerid Konstrari

Find persistent mana production when the hand contains expensive threats but insufficient acceleration. Overlord of the Hauntwoods is another ramp target when its land output fits better.

→
Survival of the Fittest → Druid of Purification

Find an on-theme answer to artifact or enchantment hate before attempting recursion. Against a creature problem, search the relevant removal creature instead.

→
Survival of the Fittest → Pathbreaker Ibex

Find the finisher when a large creature and a developed board can attack. Do not fetch it simply because it is powerful if the current board cannot produce meaningful combat damage.

→
Demonic Tutor → Living Death

Search for the reset or finisher only after comparing graveyards and checking whether you can protect it. If opposing graveyards are dangerous, Lantern may be the actual missing card.

Emeritus of Woe // Demonic Tutor

A four-mana creature that arrives prepared and lets you pay one generic and one black to cast its Demonic Tutor spell copy. Blitz discounts the creature, not the prepared spell's mana cost. Budget for both; the sorcery copy still needs sorcery timing. Becoming prepared again requires two creatures to have died before the end step begins, and it matters only if Emeritus stays around to use it.

→
Birthing Pod → Overlord of the Hauntwoods

Sacrifice a four-mana creature to find this five-mana ramp threat. The same rung offers Terror of the Peaks for a coming reanimation turn or Junji for recovery. Choose the actual job rather than climbing blindly.

→
Birthing Pod → Pathbreaker Ibex

Sacrifice a five-mana creature for the six-mana finisher, but it needs haste from Chainer if you want to attack immediately after Pod finds it. Kokusho and Ziatora are other six-mana options when drain or sacrifice value is more appropriate.

Opening hands and sequencing

Keep three lands with a credible route to black-red-green, a one-mana accelerator, and a useful four- or five-mana creature. A representative keep is Verdant Catacombs, Copperline Gorge, Command Tower, Birds of Paradise, Aerid Konstrari, Greater Good, and Assassin's Trophy. Fetch for green first while planning the black and red needed for Henzie; then turn-two Henzie can lead into turn-three blitz development.

Two-land hands need reliable cheap acceleration and a plausible path to the next land. Mulligan hands full of seven- and eight-mana threats with no setup, or hands that count Cradle as dependable mana before a creature survives. The two modal lands help reach 37 potential land slots, but choosing those land faces gives up their spell effects.

Each turn, ask whether the creature should be blitzed or cast normally. Persistent engines such as Meren and Terror often deserve to stay; an entry-and-attack threat is an excellent blitz candidate. Do not sacrifice Henzie merely to increase the discount unless the future savings justify commander tax and lost tempo. The discount counts command-zone casts, not deaths.

At the end step, stack Ritual and Ziatora to use a blitz creature before its own sacrifice resolves. Meren's target must already be in the graveyard; Avacyn needs deaths before the step begins for same-turn returns. With several effects, decide the intended sacrifice and recursion order before placing triggers on the stack.

Graveyard exile, entry-trigger suppression, and repeated removal of Henzie are the major pressure points. Keep removal for the hate piece that stops your hand, preserve an engine instead of overcommitting every threat, and use the land-ramp creatures to make hard-casting viable after commander tax rises. The deck can grind without Henzie, but loses much of its speed and automatic death draw.
