Your deals should buy time and profitable attacks while Breena builds a board that can win without anyone's cooperation. The critical endgame rule: with only one opponent remaining, Breena cannot compare that opponent's life with another opponent's life, so its ability stops triggering. Build your finishing threat before the table becomes a duel.

Offer rewards for attacking elsewhere
Breena, the Demagogue

rewards a player for attacking an opponent of yours who has more life than another opponent of yours. Your own life total is irrelevant. You can receive the draw when you are the attacker; other attackers draw for their own qualifying attacks, while you always place the counters on a creature you control.

Breena checks the life comparison again as the trigger resolves. Attacking several qualifying opponents produces a trigger for each such opponent, not for each attacking creature. Connecting is unnecessary, and creatures put onto the battlefield attacking do not create additional attack triggers. At a four-player table, your own split attack can produce two triggers if two opponents have more life than the third.

Duelist's Heritage

offer double strike to an opponent's attacker aimed at the player you both need to weaken. The trigger happens on every player's attack, but only gives one creature double strike per trigger. Once your own counter-heavy flyer is ready, the same card becomes a finisher.

Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor

rewards each creature that actually connects with one of your opponents: its controller can pay a life to draw. This encourages attacks away from you and turns your flying tokens into a draw engine. It helps opponents too, especially those with wide boards.

Firemane Commando

another explicit bargain: opponents drawing from attacks must attack with at least two creatures and send none of them at you. Your own two-creature attacks draw regardless of where they go.

Combat Calligrapher

adds a flying Inkling to qualifying attacks at your opponents, including your own attacks. While Calligrapher remains on the battlefield, all Inklings are barred from attacking you and your planeswalkers. If it leaves, opponents keep their tokens and that restriction ends.

Shadrix Silverquill

choose two different modes for two different players at the beginning of your combat. Give yourself counters or a flyer and offer someone else the less threatening benefit. Giving an opponent the draw-and-lose-one mode can break equal life totals before attackers are declared.

Forbidden Orchard

fixing that also supplies a negotiating chip. Give the Spirit to a player who needs a blocker or a body to attack someone else, not automatically to the strongest token or sacrifice deck. The Spirit has no restriction against attacking you.

Counters in exchange for safety
Orzhov Advokist

each player chooses whether to take two counters during your upkeep. Opponents who accept cannot attack you or your planeswalkers until your next turn. They can still cast spells against you. You can accept your own counters and attack your opponents normally.

Noble Heritage

works from the ninety-nine by granting an ability to your commander. With it already present, Breena entering triggers the offer; later your upkeep does too. Opponents who accept give YOU protection from that player until your next turn. Your creatures are not protected, and accepting does not prohibit attacking you, although ordinary damage to you from that player is prevented.

Nils, Discipline Enforcer

place a counter on your own creature and optionally one creature per opponent at your end step. Their growing creatures become more expensive to send at you: the tax counts all counters on each attacking creature, not just +1/+1 counters. It is a payment option, not a ban on attacking.

Ghostly Prison

turns your deals into a practical choice: pay two mana per creature to attack you, or spend that mana developing while attacking elsewhere. It does not protect planeswalkers, and opponents can still choose to pay.

Charismatic Conqueror

every opposing creature or artifact entering untapped poses a decision: tap it, or give you a lifelink Vampire. Those tokens become blockers, Breena counter recipients, and part of your eventual finishing attack. A permanent that enters tapped never triggers it.

Mangara, the Diplomat

draws when an opponent sends at least two creatures at you or your planeswalkers, and also when an opponent casts their second spell in a turn. It makes ignoring your incentives productive for you, but does not actually prevent an attack.

Bargains that produce resources
Master of Ceremonies

every opponent chooses a Treasure, a Citizen, or a card during your upkeep, and you receive a matching benefit for every choice. At a four-player table you receive three benefits even if nobody chooses cards. Explain the choices together, then let each player choose what they need.

Tenuous Truce

choose an opponent who benefits from developing without attacking you. You both draw at that player's end step while the truce survives. Either player attacking the other or their planeswalker breaks it, but spell-based aggression does not automatically break it.

Loran of the Third Path

remove a dangerous artifact or enchantment on entry, then offer a shared draw when another player needs to find an answer. Its draw ability requires a legal opponent target, and you should choose the beneficiary deliberately each time.

Secret Rendezvous

three cards each is a substantial bargain, not free advantage. Offer it to a player behind on resources who can help stop the leader. It is particularly dangerous to give three cards to a combo player with open mana; keeping it in hand can be correct.

Tempt with Bunnies

each opponent can accept a card and Rabbit, giving you another card and Rabbit for each acceptance. You get one of each even if everyone refuses. Do not count on drawing four when deciding whether an opening hand functions.

Wedding Ring

choose a partner whose natural draw engine benefits you and whose extra cards are unlikely to immediately end the game. You copy their draws and life gain during THEIR turn; they copy yours during YOUR turn. A draw during another player's turn does not automatically trigger this pair, and it is not an infinite draw loop.

Pendant of Prosperity

give it to an opponent who needs lands and cards. Their two-mana activation gives both them and you a draw and an optional land from hand onto the battlefield. The recipient controls whether it is used, so this is negotiated value rather than dependable ramp.

Victory Chimes

offer one colorless mana to help another player pay for a useful answer, or keep it for your own interaction. It untaps during each other player's untap step. Mana empties as steps and phases end, so agree on the timing before providing it; you cannot reclaim a loan after it is spent.

Oft-Nabbed Goat

a deliberately interactive prize: only opponents of its current controller may pay one mana at sorcery speed to draw, take it, and add a -1/-1 counter. Nobody can repeatedly activate it while they control it. When it dies, its OWNER draws for the -1/-1 counters and each other player loses that much life, regardless of who controlled it.

Do not load the Goat with your +1/+1 counters. Opposite counter types cancel through state-based actions, reducing its eventual payout. Likewise, avoid Nils putting counters on the Goat while an opponent controls it. Players can also refuse to take it or exile it, so its payoff is not guaranteed.

Palantír of Orthanc

ask a chosen opponent whether they will let you draw or risk losing life from your milled cards. Scry first to inform your decision about whom to target. You cannot guarantee useful draws, and its milling can put a finisher into your graveyard instead.

Benevolent Offering

choose who receives three flying Spirits and independently who receives life; they can be the same opponent or different opponents. You receive both benefits. Use it at instant speed to supply an ally with blockers, or before your own turn to build attackers. Changing an opponent's life total also changes Breena's future incentives.

Profit from the cards you hand out
+
Tataru Taru + Loran of the Third Path

activate Loran so the chosen opponent draws outside that opponent's own turn. Tataru then makes a tapped Treasure, at most once that turn. A Breena draw during that opponent's own attack does NOT trigger Tataru.

Gleaming Splendor

each opponent's second draw in a turn makes you a Treasure. An opponent's normal draw followed by Breena's attack reward is enough. Its activated ability gives two targeted players a card; you may choose yourself and a useful ally. Unlike Tataru, its trigger cares about the second draw, not whose turn it is.

Smothering Tithe

shared draws become mana unless each drawing opponent pays two for each card. This is a real cost to your allies, so make the offer honestly. Do not promise that accepting a draw will provide you no benefit when Tithe is present.

Lotho, Corrupt Shirriff

gives a Treasure and costs you a life whenever any player casts their second spell in a turn. It is useful independent ramp when the table declines the more generous deals.

Esper Sentinel

a strong early counter recipient. Increasing its power raises the payment needed to stop its draw trigger, making Breena's counters useful before you are ready to attack for lethal.

Dusk Legion Duelist

putting Breena's counters here adds your own draw even when another player received Breena's attack reward. Duelist triggers only once each turn, so distribute later counters elsewhere after it has already drawn that turn.

+
Deep Gnome Terramancer + Knight of the White Orchid

independent land acceleration that does not require an ally's permission. Both find Plains cards, including typed duals; Gnome's land enters tapped and its ability is usable once per turn, while Knight requires an opponent to have more lands as its trigger resolves.

The five cheap rocks support turn-three Breena and your four-mana political engines. The mana base has 36 lands plus three modal lands, with enough white sources for the double-white cards and enough typed lands for the Plains searches. Keep utility lands in proportion: several important spells need two colored mana.

Put choices on the removal spells too
Council's Judgment

a genuine table vote that exiles every permanent tied for the most votes. Vote for a meaningful threat and discuss whether opponents want to cooperate on one target or spread votes. It does not target, so hexproof does not stop the chosen permanent being exiled. You cannot guarantee that everyone votes as promised.

Promise of Loyalty

everyone chooses one creature to keep and sacrifices the rest. Opposing survivors cannot attack you or your planeswalkers while they retain their vow counters. Your survivor can still attack opponents. Later creatures and creatures whose vow counters are removed are not restricted.

Magister of Worth

negotiate before casting this six-mana swing. Grace reanimates EVERY player's creature graveyard; condemnation or a tied vote destroys every other creature. In a four-player vote, your condemnation vote plus one ally is enough for at least a tie. Do not promise a safe wipe if that ally is free to change their vote.

Damn

the dependable fallback when a vote is too risky. Cast it for targeted removal or overload it to destroy all creatures, including yours. Indestructibility can protect your board from this destruction but cannot save creatures sacrificed to Promise of Loyalty.

+
Swords to Plowshares + Path to Exile

efficient exile for creatures that must disappear now. Both compensate their controller, which can be useful in a deal, but neither requires an opponent to accept the removal. The life from Swords can also change which opponent qualifies for Breena.

+
Anguished Unmaking + Generous Gift

broad instant-speed answers when the threatening permanent is not a creature. Save one for a card that would otherwise invalidate your bargains or end the game; the deck cannot negotiate with a resolved winning combo.

Reprieve

returns a spell to hand and replaces itself, buying a window to attack or assemble a real answer. It can return an uncounterable spell, but the opponent may cast the spell again if they have the mana and legal timing.

+
Soul-Guide Lantern + Bojuka Bog

keep graveyard decks from converting the long political game into a sudden win. Lantern's sacrifice exiles EVERY opponent's graveyard, including an ally's; Bog targets just one player. Announce that distinction when negotiating.

End the game when the bargains have done their job
+
Breena, the Demagogue + Duelist's Heritage

ten +1/+1 counters make Breena an 11-power flyer; double strike then threatens 22 commander combat damage to one player if both hits connect. Counters from several engines contribute. Track commander damage separately for each opponent and ensure you can get through blockers.

Shadrix Silverquill

a naturally evasive double-striker that converts counters into ordinary damage quickly. It is not your commander, so its damage does not count toward Breena's 21-damage threshold. It remains useful after Breena's reward stops in a duel.

Akroma's Will

with Breena present when you cast it, choose both modes to turn your counter-heavy creatures and flying tokens into a protected double-striking attack. Without a commander, choose the mode that actually solves the board. It is both a finisher and emergency protection, not an automatic win on an empty board.

Inkshield

converts combat damage that would hit YOU into flying Inklings while preventing that damage. It does not save your creatures or planeswalkers from combat damage. The tokens normally attack on your next turn, so plan how to protect them until then.

Court of Grace

introduces the monarch as another reason to attack and supplies flying bodies every upkeep. Keep the crown when safely possible for 4/4 Angels, but giving it up temporarily can redirect combat and keep you out of the lead. It still makes a Spirit if you are not the monarch.

+
Rogue's Passage + Vault of the Archangel

Passage forces a large creature through blockers; Vault turns a wide attack into a life swing and makes blocks expensive through deathtouch. These activations require substantial mana, so do not leave yourself unable to protect the finishing threat.

Preserve the advantage you negotiated
+
The Ozolith + Felisa, Fang of Silverquill

when a counter-bearing nontoken creature dies, Ozolith records its counters and Felisa creates that many tapped flying Inklings. These effects can both benefit from the same death. Ozolith also works on other ways a creature leaves; Felisa specifically needs a death and does not trigger from tokens.

+
Mother of Runes + Giver of Runes

protect a key creature or make it difficult for creatures of the chosen color to block. Mother can target herself; Giver cannot. Their tap abilities require them to have been under your control since your turn began unless they gain haste.

Lightning Greaves

protects Breena from targeting, but shroud also prevents YOUR Breena triggers and Duelist's Heritage from targeting it. Move the Greaves to another creature before declaring attacks if Breena needs counters or targeted double strike.

+
Dawn's Truce + Selfless Spirit

Truce can grant your permanents indestructible if you promise the card gift, while Spirit protects your creatures from destruction. Neither stops sacrifice or toughness reduction; Truce's hexproof only stops opponents targeting you or your permanents.

Teferi's Protection

preserves both you and your board through a decisive attack or reset. Phasing out is not leaving the battlefield and does not bank counters on Ozolith. Your absence may change what incentives opponents have, so consider the whole table before spending it.

+
Sun Titan + Sevinne's Reclamation

recover low-cost political engines, protection, and mana after a wipe. Titan can repeatedly return permanents with mana value three or less; Reclamation's flashback can recover two different eligible permanents. Neither returns a four- or five-mana creature.

++
Agadeem's Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt + Witch Enchanter // Witch-Blessed Meadow + Sejiri Shelter // Sejiri Glacier

modal land slots become recovery, artifact/enchantment removal, or targeted protection when you already have enough mana. Awakening returns creatures of DIFFERENT mana values, not every creature costing X or less. Use the land faces early when missing a land drop would delay your engine.

Find the tool the current negotiation needs
→
Enlightened Tutor → Duelist's Heritage

find the finishing double strike once you have a threatening attacker, or the combat bargaining tool when opponents are willing to cooperate. Tutor puts the card on top, not into your hand; a later Loran activation can draw it immediately.

→
Enlightened Tutor → Noble Heritage

find the recurring counter offer when your commander is present and accepting opponents' damage is the immediate problem. If casting it after Breena, you wait until your next upkeep for the first trigger unless Breena enters again.

→
Enlightened Tutor → Smothering Tithe

find mana when your shared-draw engines are active but expensive interaction and finishers are stranded. Remember you still need a draw and four mana to deploy it.

→
Wishclaw Talisman → Akroma's Will

find a finisher when your board can end the game before the recipient gets a meaningful tutor turn. If the game will continue, choose a recipient who needs an answer to a mutual threat and recognize that their promise is not mechanically binding.

→
Wishclaw Talisman → Inkshield

find the defensive reversal when an overwhelming attack is likely. Giving another player a tutor can make that plan fail if they find protection or a combo instead, so do not use this line casually. A recipient cannot activate Wishclaw during your turn.

Opening hands and sequencing

Look for three lands, a cheap rock or land accelerator, and either an early body or a political engine. White-heavy hands are usually good, but ensure black is available for Breena. Avoid hands that depend on an opponent activating Pendant or accepting Tempt with Bunnies to find their next land.

An ideal start develops an early creature and mana, then Breena while there is still room to direct combat. At equal life totals Breena does nothing, so let a small attack or a suitable life-changing effect establish the comparison. Do not attack the lowest-life opponent automatically just because that player is easiest to hit; attacking a qualifying opponent may provide both a card and counters.

Offer specific, short deals: double strike for an attacker aimed at the leader, a shared draw to look for an answer, or counters under Advokist's printed restriction. The game's rules enforce the printed restrictions, not verbal promises. Keep gifts small enough that a broken promise does not immediately cost you the game, and follow through on deals you intend people to trust later.

Spread counters between a draw engine and an evasive threat until you are ready to finish. Announcing every potential commander-damage kill too early makes you the obvious target; hiding legal game information is unnecessary. When eliminating a player, check whether you are also switching off Breena by leaving only one opponent.

This is a political combat deck, not a combo deck. Expect a slower game than a dedicated aggressive Breena build, especially against tables that refuse bargains. Against spell-combo decks, stop subsidizing their cards, preserve real interaction, and use your flying clock. Against creature tables, your incentives and counter growth should do much more of the work.
