Gaddock Teeg turns off the top half of every noncreature card in the format: no wrath, no Cyclonic Rift, no Torment of Hailfire, no big X draw spell, no Approach of the Second Sun. The deck is built so that constraint is entirely one-sided — every removal effect here is either stapled to a creature or costs three or less, so you keep answering things while the table cannot.

The commander
Gaddock Teeg

two mana. No noncreature spell with mana value four or more can be cast by anyone, and no noncreature spell with {X} in its cost at all. He is a 2/2, so protect him; he is also the single most warping card the deck has.

Note what he does NOT stop: creature spells of any size, and permanents already on the battlefield. That is why the deck's own threats are all creatures.

Conqueror's Flail

opponents cannot cast spells during your turn at all while it is attached. Teeg stops the expensive answers; the Flail stops the cheap ones.

Grand Abolisher

the same lockout on a body.

Dosan the Falling Leaf

players can cast spells only during their own turns. With Teeg out, an opponent's window to answer anything shrinks to their own main phase, at three mana or less.

City of Solitude

the enchantment version, harder to remove.

Removal that survives your own lock

Every one of these is a creature or costs three or less, which is the whole design constraint.

Aura Shards

the engine. Every creature you play destroys an artifact or enchantment, and Teeg means they cannot wrath your creatures away to stop it.

Acidic Slime

an artifact, enchantment OR land, on a deathtouch blocker.

Bane of Progress

destroys EVERY artifact and enchantment on the board and grows for each. Your own board is creatures, so it is close to one-sided.

Knight of Autumn

removal, life, or a 4/3 — pick what the board needs.

Duplicant

exiles a creature permanently and becomes it.

Meteor Golem

destroys any nonland permanent. Seven mana, but it is a creature, so Teeg lets you cast it.

Path to Exile

one mana, exile.

Get Lost

two mana for a creature, enchantment or planeswalker.

Generous Gift

three mana for literally any permanent.

Beast Within

the instant-speed version.

Krosan Grip

split second, so it beats the response.

Nature's Claim

one mana for any artifact or enchantment.

Aura of Silence

taxes their artifacts and enchantments, then eats one.

The rest of the lock
Archon of Emeria

one spell per turn each, and their nonbasics enter tapped.

Drannith Magistrate

no casting from graveyards, exile or the command zone.

Linvala, Keeper of Silence

their creature abilities stop working.

Collector Ouphe

their artifact abilities stop working.

Kataki, War's Wage

every artifact costs its controller {1} a turn to keep. Against an artifact deck it is a slow Armageddon.

Containment Priest

anything entering without being cast is exiled — reanimation, blink and cheat-into-play all stop.

Grafdigger's Cage

nothing comes back from graveyards or libraries.

Aven Mindcensor

flash him onto a fetchland or tutor.

Archivist of Oghma

a card and a life every time anyone searches.

Esper Sentinel

taxes their first noncreature spell each turn.

Ghostly Prison

they pay per attacker, which matters because Teeg is a 2/2 and the deck is slow to block.

Cards
Beast Whisperer

a card for every creature spell, which is most of the deck.

Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines

your enter triggers happen twice and theirs do not happen at all. It doubles Aura Shards, Acidic Slime, Bane of Progress, Knight of Autumn and Craterhoof simultaneously.

Archivist of Oghma

cards off their tutors.

How you actually win

This is the question a Teeg deck has to answer, and the answer is that everything lethal here is a creature.

Craterhoof Behemoth

the standard ending on a board of hatebears.

End-Raze Forerunners

the second copy, with vigilance and haste for the whole team.

Avenger of Zendikar

a Plant for every land you control, then counters on all of them per landfall.

Adeline, Resplendent Cathar

a body per opponent on every attack, and her power counts the whole board.

Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite

+2/+2 to yours and -2/-2 to theirs, which is a one-sided wrath that keeps attacking afterwards.

What to keep in an opening hand

Keep a mana dork and Teeg. Turn-one acceleration into a turn-two Teeg is the strongest start in the deck, and every turn he is out is a turn the table cannot cast its best cards.

Protect him over developing. Teeg is a 2/2 that every opponent wants dead, and the lock is worth far more than one extra hatebear on the battlefield.

Aura Shards

prioritise it. Teeg already stops them casting the wrath that would clear your creatures, so Aura Shards becomes a removal spell on every single creature you play for the rest of the game.

Remember Teeg binds you too. There are no X spells and no expensive noncreature cards in the deck on purpose — if you ever want one, it has to be a creature.
