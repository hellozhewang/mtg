Do not spend your whole early game trying to cast the eight-mana commander. Build mana, establish blockers, and resolve a Bolas planeswalker first. The original Nicol Bolas is the final threat that strips a defender's hand; the deck's draw, removal, and wins work without him.

This is a Bolas story deck for Bracket 3: all six distinct Nicol Bolas cards, his servants, his corrupted gods, and his signature spells. It prioritizes that identity over efficiency. Its 38 lands and 12 mana rocks support an expensive curve, but three opponents attacking together can still overwhelm it. The three Game Changers are Bolas's Citadel, Cyclonic Rift, and Rhystic Study. It has no built-in infinite loop or extra-turn engine. Archenemy describes the table role here; this remains ordinary four-player Commander, without a separate scheme deck.

The many faces of the God-Pharaoh

Nicol Bolas

Your commander is the original legendary creature, not a planeswalker. His damage trigger empties the damaged opponent's hand even when the damage is not combat damage, making Sarkhan the Mad a real partner. Budget {U}{B}{R} for each upkeep or accept sacrificing him. Seven unmodified combat damage needs three hits for a commander-damage kill; noncombat damage does not count toward that rule.

Nicol Bolas, the Ravager // Nicol Bolas, the Arisen

The cheapest Bolas body strips a card from every opponent and can block flyers. Transforming costs seven mana at sorcery speed; after it resolves, the planeswalker side draws two, destroys large threats with damage, or reanimates a creature or planeswalker from any graveyard. Its library-exiling ultimate leaves one card, so it does not cause an immediate loss on resolution.

Nicol Bolas, Dragon-God

The main alternate-win threat also draws cards and erodes every opponent's resources. His -8 only defeats opponents who control neither a legendary creature nor a planeswalker when it resolves. Clear those permanents first. Borrowing every other planeswalker's loyalty abilities does not give him extra activations; The Chain Veil is the card that provides those.

Nicol Bolas, God-Pharaoh

The +2 steals spells, the +1 strips two cards from each opponent, and the -4 removes a threat or finishes a weakened player. His -12 exiles all opposing nonland permanents, clearing the way for your armies or Dragon-God's ultimate. The free spell from +2 still follows normal timing restrictions and must be cast that turn.

Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker

Use +3 against threatening nonland engines and -2 to take the best attacker or blocker permanently. The creature theft does not grant haste. His -9 devastates one player, but the victim chooses the seven permanents to sacrifice; it is not a precise removal spell. This deck uses him for theft and nonland control rather than repeated attacks on mana bases.

Nicol Bolas, the Deceiver

Eight mana is inefficient, but this incarnation earns its slot on theme. The +3 pressures all opponents while building loyalty, and the -3 removes a creature while replacing itself with a card. The -11 deals seven to every opponent and draws seven; The Locust God turns those actual draws into seven flying, hasty attackers.

Visage of Bolas

Four-mana ramp is a deliberate flavor concession. It also finds Nicol Bolas, the Deceiver in your library or graveyard, so it supplies both mana and a late threat. It cannot search for the other Bolas cards.

Dark Intimations

Each opponent sacrifices a creature or planeswalker and discards, while you recover a creature or planeswalker to hand and draw. Its graveyard trigger gives the next Bolas planeswalker spell you cast an extra starting loyalty counter. Casting the Ravager's creature face or transforming it does not trigger that bonus.

Steal sparks and reach the ultimate

The Elderspell

Convert opposing planeswalkers, or your own expendable servants, into two loyalty per planeswalker actually destroyed. A fresh Dragon-God starts at four, so destroying two other planeswalkers can bring him to eight. That line needs those additional permanents and a clear opposing legendary-creature board; the two named spells alone are not a guaranteed win. Do not activate Dragon-God first unless The Chain Veil will allow another activation.

Deepglow Skate

Double loyalty on several planeswalkers at once, and grow an Army alongside them. A fresh God-Pharaoh goes from seven to fourteen and can use -12 immediately if he has not already activated that turn. Dragon-God goes from four to eight, but still needs his ultimate's board condition. Doubling loyalty does not reset the activation limit.

The Chain Veil

Spend four and tap it for an additional loyalty activation from each planeswalker you control. On a developed board, this can mean another draw, removal spell, or finishing ultimate from several cards at once. There is no repeatable untap engine here: these are finite turns, and the four-mana activation competes with protection and new threats.

Flux Channeler

Casting rocks, planeswalkers, and the deck's many noncreature spells grows your existing loyalty and Army counters. The proliferate trigger resolves before the spell that caused it, so a newly cast planeswalker does not benefit from its own cast trigger. Keep this creature back as support rather than trading it in an incidental attack.

Spark Double

Copy your best Bolas planeswalker without the legend rule removing either one, and enter with one additional loyalty counter. It can instead copy a useful creature such as The Locust God. Two Bolas planeswalkers give The Chain Veil and Deepglow Skate much better returns than simply casting another unsupported threat.

Tezzeret's Gambit

Draw two while advancing every planeswalker and Army you choose. Paying two life instead of blue reduces the normal mana payment to three. If you cast it from the top through Bolas's Citadel instead, that alternative cost is four life because its mana value remains four.

Contagion Clasp

Marks a creature with a -1/-1 counter, then offers repeatable proliferation when you have spare mana. It can advance loyalty, increase an Army, and worsen opposing -1/-1 counters in the same activation. The Scorpion God rewards a marked creature eventually dying.

Karn's Bastion

A land slot becomes a late loyalty accelerator. Its activation costs four mana plus tapping the Bastion itself, so it normally occupies five mana sources. Activate before using a planeswalker's loyalty ability when that extra counter unlocks the ultimate.

Build the Dreadhorde and corrupted gods

Dreadhorde Invasion

Supplies a recurring Army blocker and grows it on each of your upkeeps. Repeated amass usually adds counters to one Army rather than producing a wide board. A Zombie token attacking with power at least six gains lifelink for the turn, which helps repay the life spent on Bolas's Citadel and Command the Dreadhorde.

Gleaming Overseer

Creates or enlarges the Army and gives your Zombie tokens hexproof and menace. That protects both the Army and The Scarab God's token copies from targeted opposing removal. It does not give your nontoken creatures or planeswalkers hexproof, and it does not stop sweepers.

Vizier of the Scorpion

Deathtouch makes even a small Zombie token a serious deterrent to attackers. More dramatically, it makes Widespread Brutality's Army damage lethal to ordinary non-Army creatures. The Vizier itself will usually die in that sweep, which is an acceptable cost for protecting your planeswalkers.

The Scarab God

Turn the best creature cards in any graveyard into your own 4/4 Zombies. The upkeep drain counts Zombie creatures, not their power, so one huge Army only contributes one. Your Zombie support creatures and additional tokens make that drain scale across all three opponents, while the scry helps manage Bolas's Citadel's top card.

The Locust God

Turns your actual card draws into flying blockers or hasty attackers. Disciple of Bolas, Cruel Ultimatum, and the Deceiver's ultimate can make several Insects at once. Cards merely put into your hand, such as Deliver Unto Evil's returns or Sarkhan the Mad's 0, do not trigger it.

The Scorpion God

The least efficient of the three corrupted gods is included to complete the theme, and still provides removal and draw. Put -1/-1 counters on opposing creatures before a sweep to draw when they die; Contagion Clasp can supply another mark. All three corrupted gods return to hand at the next end step after dying, not immediately, and exile removal bypasses that death trigger.

Keep the throne while everyone attacks

Hour of Devastation

Removes indestructible from creatures and deals five to every creature and every non-Bolas planeswalker. Your Bolas planeswalkers are spared, but Liliana, both Tezzerets, Sarkhan, and many of your own creatures are not. The original commander is a 7/7 creature and survives five damage if otherwise healthy; the untransformed Ravager is a 4/4 and normally dies.

Widespread Brutality

Amass first, then your chosen Army deals its power to every non-Army creature. With Vizier of the Scorpion present, that damage has deathtouch even though the Vizier also takes damage and usually dies. Planeswalkers are untouched. Indestructible creatures can survive, opposing Armies are excluded, and your own non-Army creatures are hit too.

Crux of Fate

Usually choose non-Dragons to preserve the commander and untransformed Ravager while clearing attackers. It does not touch planeswalkers, but does destroy your non-Dragon support and gods unless another effect protects them. Choose the Dragon mode only when opposing Dragons are the greater threat.

Damnation

The cheaper full creature reset for a board that has outgrown your themed damage sweepers. Planeswalkers and mana rocks survive, so cast it when that exchange leaves you ahead. It destroys rather than exiles and cannot remove indestructible creatures.

Cyclonic Rift

The seven-mana overload clears all opposing nonland permanents at instant speed. When Dragon-God has eight loyalty and an activation available, overload during your own main phase, then activate -8. Opponents can still respond by flashing in a legendary creature or disrupting the planeswalker or ability; the line is powerful, not unanswerable. Returning cards to hands also replenishes discard choices for Torment of Hailfire, so Rift is primarily the ultimate or combat setup.

Ripples of Potential

Proliferate your threatened planeswalkers or Army, then phase out the chosen permanents that received counters. This preserves them through many sweepers while retaining their counters. Ordinary rocks and creatures without counters cannot be protected this way. You may leave a proliferated permanent on the battlefield if you need it now.

Lazotep Plating

Protects you and your current permanents from opposing targeted effects while adding an Army counter or blocker. It is especially useful when an opponent tries to remove the planeswalker that is about to ultimate. Hexproof does not answer a nontargeting board wipe or protect your spells on the stack.

Finish with domination, armies, or a cruel choice

Torment of Hailfire

A direct multiplayer finisher after Bolas has reduced hands and nonland boards. With no cards or expendable nonland permanents, an opponent loses 3X life; otherwise their sacrifices and discards reduce that damage. Count those resources before committing all your mana. The rocks let you cast a meaningful X without needing an infinite-mana combo.

Tezzeret, Master of the Bridge

Your many rocks become a source of table-wide damage and life gain through +2. His affinity reduces only generic costs on creature and planeswalker spells: it helps the expensive Bolas incarnations, but cannot reduce Dragon-God's entirely colored cost or the Ravager's transform activation. The life gain supports Citadel and reanimation, while his -3 recovers a destroyed key artifact.

Tezzeret, Agent of Bolas

Look for mana rocks, The Chain Veil, or Bolas's Citadel with +1; the fifteen-artifact deck can still miss. His -4 drains one player for twice your artifact count and gains that much life. Use the 5/5 animation when you need a blocker or attacker, remembering that the animated rock becomes vulnerable to creature sweepers.

Sarkhan the Mad

His -4 lets the original Nicol Bolas deal seven noncombat damage to a chosen player and trigger the commander's hand discard. Additional Dragons add damage, but only damage from your original commander causes its discard trigger. His -2 trades a creature for a Dragon; his 0 puts a card into hand without drawing and can cost substantial loyalty in this expensive deck.

In Bolas's Clutches

Take a creature, planeswalker, or other important permanent permanently for as long as the Aura remains attached. Stealing the last legendary creature an opponent controls can expose them to Dragon-God's ultimate. The stolen permanent becomes legendary, so consider the legend rule if you already control another legendary permanent with the same name.

Slave of Bolas

Borrow a dangerous creature with haste, use it, and sacrifice it at the next end step. In your second main phase, Disciple of Bolas can sacrifice the borrowed creature for life and cards before the scheduled sacrifice. Removing a key legendary defender this way also helps set up Dragon-God.

Refill your hand and raise the fallen

Bolas's Citadel

Converts life into spells from the top of your library, including Bolas planeswalkers. This deck has expensive spells, so treat it as a burst of advantage rather than assuming you can cast the whole library. A land on top can stop you once your land play is used; fetchlands, draw effects, and The Scarab God's scry help. Ten expendable nonland permanents, often including Insects and spent rocks, can be sacrificed for ten life lost by each opponent. Keep a winning board if that activation would not finish the job.

Rhystic Study

The early draw engine that lets this intentionally expensive theme survive a three-opponent game. Deploy it before the high-cost threats when possible. The draws also trigger The Locust God later, but the deck still needs its other draw sources when opponents pay.

Soul Diviner

Converts a spare counter into a card each turn. Dreadhorde Invasion and proliferate can replenish Army counters, and surplus loyalty is another resource. Removing the last +1/+1 counter from an otherwise 0/0 Army kills it; removing the last loyalty counter similarly loses the planeswalker. Do not consume the counter that your next ultimate needs.

Disciple of Bolas

Cash in a large Army, a stolen creature, or the commander when it is worth more as life and cards. Sacrificing an unmodified original Bolas yields seven of each, then you may move the commander from the graveyard to the command zone. The life fuels Citadel or Command the Dreadhorde, and The Locust God turns the draws into replacement bodies.

Cruel Ultimatum

This signature spell punishes one opponent while recovering a creature, drawing three, and gaining five life. It is expensive and only targets one opponent, so choose the player whose hand and blocker matter most. Tezzeret, Master of the Bridge cannot discount this sorcery, and its entirely colored cost demands careful land sequencing.

Deliver Unto Evil

With any Bolas planeswalker on your battlefield, recover up to four targeted cards of any types from your graveyard. Without one, an opponent chooses two of the targeted cards to leave behind, so the return is much smaller. The original creature commander and untransformed Ravager do not satisfy the planeswalker condition. This spell exiles itself after resolving.

Command the Dreadhorde

Rebuild your planeswalkers and steal creatures or planeswalkers from any graveyards in one spell. Add up the total mana value before selecting targets: it deals that much damage to you before returning them. Do not plan on a returning creature saving you from lethal damage through a later enters trigger. Keep enough life to survive and defend the resulting board.

The Eldest Reborn

Each chapter advances the villain plan: pressure boards, strip hands, then take a creature or planeswalker from any graveyard. Proliferation can accelerate the chapters, but putting several lore counters on at once can trigger several chapters together rather than creating leisurely turns between them. Choose the chapter-three target when its trigger is put on the stack.

Liliana, Death's Majesty

Creates Zombie blockers while milling resources for your graveyard recovery. Her -3 can return the original commander if you left him in the graveyard, a corrupted god, or Deepglow Skate. The returned creature is also a Zombie, improving The Scarab God's count. Her -7 preserves Zombies but destroys your other non-Zombie creatures; a planeswalker returned by other cards is not affected by that creature sweep.

Commence the Endgame

Hold up interaction, then draw two and create or enlarge an Army at the end of the preceding opponent's turn. The amount amassed uses your hand size after drawing. The spell cannot be countered, but the resulting Army can still be removed afterward, and it needs evasion or a clear board to connect.

Enter the God-Eternals

Removes a smaller creature, gains life based on damage actually dealt, mills a chosen player, and amasses four. Mill yourself when looking for reanimation targets or an opponent when The Scarab God can exploit their creatures. If the creature target is illegal but the player target remains legal, the spell can still resolve its other effects; you do not gain life from damage it never dealt.

Stop the answer that would end your reign

Archenemy's Charm

This newer support card fills a real gap: instant-speed exile for a creature or planeswalker, or recovery of up to two creature/planeswalker cards to hand. Its lifelink mode can turn a commander or Army attack into a Citadel refill. Triple black makes early fetch choices important.

Counterspell

Save unconditional permission for a decisive opposing win or the answer to your finishing planeswalker. Spending it on a minor value spell is costly when three players can still threaten you. Keep two blue available before tapping out for a spectacle.

Arcane Denial

Easier to hold up than Counterspell when your mana is stretched across three colors. It replaces itself but gives the countered spell's controller up to two cards next upkeep, so use it when stopping the spell is worth restoring that opponent's resources.

Swan Song

One-mana protection against an instant, sorcery, or enchantment is valuable alongside an expensive planeswalker. It cannot stop a creature or planeswalker spell. The gifted flying Bird can attack your planeswalkers later, so remember the blocker requirement it creates.

Feed the Swarm

Answers an opposing enchantment that would shut off your engines, or a creature if necessary. It is sorcery speed and costs life equal to the removed permanent's mana value. Preserve life when Citadel or Command the Dreadhorde is your next major play.

Pay for the empire

Chromatic Lantern

The mana base already has original duals, shocks, fetches, and multiplayer lands; Lantern adds insurance for Cruel Ultimatum and Dragon-God rather than replacing good fixing. It also lets your utility lands produce needed colors. Removing it can still disrupt a carefully planned multispell turn, so count the colors your lands naturally produce.

Coalition Relic

Store a charge on an earlier turn, then get that stored mana plus the normal tap mana in your next first main phase. Proliferation can add another charge while one is present. All charge counters are removed by the first-main trigger, and the resulting mana does not last indefinitely through later phases.

Interplanar Beacon

Gains a life when you cast a planeswalker, helping with the deck's incidental life payments. Its filter produces two different colors only for planeswalker spells; it does not cast the creature commander with that colored mana and cannot pay for the Ravager's transform ability. It cannot make two black toward Dragon-God by itself.

Command Beacon

Move the commander to hand when commander tax makes another cast unreasonable. You still pay his normal eight-mana cost from hand, and sacrificing a land is a real cost here. Do not spend it merely to avoid the first cast from the command zone, which has no tax.

What to keep in an opening hand and how to sequence it

Keep three or four lands, access to black and blue, an early mana rock, and either draw or an inexpensive defensive play. A land plus Sol Ring is not enough if the rest of the hand needs multiple colored symbols. Mulligan hands full of seven- and eight-mana Bolas cards with no setup, even when the theme looks perfect.

An example keep is Polluted Delta, Blood Crypt, Island, Arcane Signet, Soul Diviner, Dreadhorde Invasion, and Nicol Bolas, Dragon-God. Fetch Underground Sea if appropriate, then decide between the turn-two Signet and a blocker engine based on opposing aggression. The Signet and your next black source can complete Dragon-God's demanding cost, while Soul Diviner and Invasion give you a slower draw plan if committing him would be unsafe.

Use the first three turns to fix mana, deploy a rock, and establish draw or blockers. Fetch a tapped surveil land or Xander's Lounge when you have no immediate spell to cast; prioritize untapped sources when interaction matters. Black is needed in quantity, but Cruel Ultimatum and the commander also need double blue and double red.

In the middle game, clear attackers before casting a planeswalker whenever possible. One protected Bolas is better than three planeswalkers immediately lost in combat. Night's Whisper and Rhystic Study are early investments; Visage of Bolas is a later setup play that supplies a guaranteed Deceiver.

Before a finishing turn, count loyalty, available activations, protection mana, opposing legendary creatures, and opposing planeswalkers. Resolve The Elderspell, Deepglow Skate, or a proliferate effect before using the loyalty ability you intend to unlock. An earlier + ability consumes the normal activation, even if you subsequently gain enough counters for an ultimate; The Chain Veil is the exception available in this list.

Choose the win your board already supports: a cleared board plus Dragon-God, a God-Pharaoh reset followed by armies, artifact-count damage from Tezzeret, or Torment of Hailfire against depleted opponents. The deck is slower and more vulnerable to coordinated attacks than an optimized control list, so use its dramatic spells to end the game once you gain the advantage.
