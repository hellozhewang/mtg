Most spellslinger decks want cheap spells. This one wants EXPENSIVE ones — Y'shtola only triggers on noncreature spells of mana value three or more, and every trigger is two damage to each opponent and two life to you. Her draw clause then checks whether a player lost four or more life this turn, which means the deck's real rhythm is two big spells per turn, every turn. One trigger is damage. Two is damage and a card.

The commander
Y'shtola, Night's Blessed

vigilance on a 2/4, two damage to each opponent and two life to you per noncreature spell of mana value three or more, and a card at end step if anybody lost four or more life this turn.

Count to two. A single trigger drains each opponent for two, which is not four — so it does not draw. Two triggers in the same turn does. Sequencing two mid-cost spells into one turn is worth more than spreading them over two.

Lyse Hext

noncreature spells cost {1} less, she grows on each one, and she gains double strike once you have cast two in a turn. She is the enabler and a clock at the same time.

Alisaie Leveilleur

the SECOND spell each turn costs {2} less, which is exactly the turn pattern Y'shtola is asking for.

Alphinaud Leveilleur

a card whenever you cast your second spell each turn. He and Alisaie fetch each other, so drawing either finds the pair.

The other drain triggers
Papalymo Totolymo

one damage to each opponent and one life to you on EVERY noncreature spell, with no mana value floor. He fills the gap Y'shtola leaves on cheap spells, and he sacrifices to strip the table's biggest creatures.

Professor Onyx

two drain and two gain on every instant and sorcery you cast or copy.

Sedgemoor Witch

a Pest for every instant and sorcery, on a menacing warded body.

Circle of Power

two cards, and a Wizard token that pings each opponent on every noncreature spell.

Sanguine Bond

every point of life you gain becomes a point an opponent loses. Y'shtola alone is gaining you two a trigger, so this roughly doubles the deck's clock.

Rosa, Resolute White Mage

a counter and lifelink each combat, which with Sanguine Bond turns an attack into a drain.

Kaya, Intangible Slayer

hexproof, so she sticks, and the plus is three drained and three gained every turn.

Card flow
Rhystic Study

their turns pay for your expensive turns.

Decorum Dissertation

Dig Through Time

Frantic Search

Cryptic Command

Krile Baldesion

Quistis Trepe

Ophidian Eye

How you actually win
Approach of the Second Sun

seven mana, gain seven, then cast it again seven cards later and win outright. In a deck that stalls the board and draws this much, it is the cleanest kill available.

Aetherflux Reservoir

your life total climbs fast between Y'shtola, Papalymo and Rosa. Fifty life to any target ends a game with no combat and no targeting restrictions.

Sanguine Bond

with the Reservoir and the drain triggers, the table's life total falls on its own.

Multiversal Recruitment

a nonlegendary copy of Y'shtola or Sedgemoor Witch, twice with flashback.

Mana
Coalition Relic

fixes all three colours and stores a mana for the second big spell.

Relic of Legends

taps any legendary creature for mana, and this deck is full of them.

Fellwar Stone

usually taps for exactly what you need.

Arcane Signet

the same.

What to keep in an opening hand

Keep mana and a cost reducer. Lyse Hext or Alisaie Leveilleur is what turns "one big spell a turn" into "two big spells a turn", and two is the number that makes Y'shtola draw.

Hold interaction until your own turn when you safely can. A counterspell cast on your turn is a Y'shtola trigger and a step toward the second one; the same card cast on their turn is just a counterspell.

Y'shtola, Night's Blessed

four mana and a 2/4 with vigilance, so she blocks well and survives most sweepers aimed at bigger boards. She is worth recasting after removal — the triggers are the deck.

Approach of the Second Sun

start it once the board is stable and you can protect the second cast. Both copies must be cast FROM YOUR HAND, so Beseech the Mirror's free-cast mode does not finish it.
